Q1Transitions — ContrastMedium
Early economists assumed that individuals always act rationally to maximise their personal utility. Behavioural economists have demonstrated that cognitive biases, social pressures, and emotional states routinely lead people to make decisions that contradict their own stated preferences.
Which transition best connects the two sentences?
  • A Furthermore, behavioural economists
  • B As a result, behavioural economists
  • C In contrast, behavioural economists
  • D For instance, behavioural economists
Correct: C
S1: economists assumed rational behaviour. S2: behavioural economists show the opposite. Direct contradiction = contrast. "In contrast" signals the pivot to the opposing finding.
Q2Transitions — Cause/ResultMedium
The introduction of the printing press in the fifteenth century dramatically reduced the cost of producing books. Literacy rates across Europe rose significantly over the following two centuries.
Which transition best connects the two sentences?
  • A Nevertheless, literacy rates
  • B However, literacy rates
  • C Consequently, literacy rates
  • D Similarly, literacy rates
Correct: C
Cheaper books → higher literacy. S2 is the direct consequence of S1. "Consequently" = cause/result. A & B = contrast/concession — wrong direction. D = addition/similarity.
Q3Transitions — ConcessionHard
It must be acknowledged that remote working arrangements can reduce overhead costs and increase employee flexibility. Studies consistently show that remote workers report higher job satisfaction and are less likely to resign than their office-based counterparts.
Which transition, added to the beginning of the second sentence, best reflects the relationship?
  • A In contrast,
  • B Therefore,
  • C For example,
  • D Moreover,
Correct: D
"It must be acknowledged that..." introduces a concession/benefit. S2 adds another benefit on top. Both sentences support remote work — this is ADDITION, not contrast. "Moreover" (= furthermore/additionally) is correct. The trap: "It must be acknowledged" looks like a concession setup, but the second sentence doesn't pivot against it — it piles on more support.
Q4Transitions — ContrastMedium
The Amazon rainforest stores an estimated 150–200 billion tonnes of carbon, making it one of the most significant carbon sinks on Earth. Deforestation and land-use changes have turned large sections of the eastern Amazon into a net carbon emitter.
Which transition best connects the two sentences?
  • A As a result, deforestation
  • B Additionally, deforestation
  • C Yet deforestation
  • D For instance, deforestation
Correct: C
S1: Amazon = massive carbon absorber. S2: parts of it now EMIT carbon. Sharp contrast — the opposite function. "Yet" signals the pivot. A (result) — the deforestation didn't result from the storage capacity. B (additionally) — same direction, wrong. D (for instance) — not an example of the storage capacity.
Q5Transitions — AdditionMedium
The Mediterranean diet is associated with reduced risk of cardiovascular disease, with studies showing lower rates of heart attack and stroke among adherents. Research links it to improved cognitive function and a reduced incidence of neurodegenerative conditions such as Alzheimer's disease.
Which transition best connects the two sentences?
  • A However, research
  • B For example, research
  • C Furthermore, research
  • D Nevertheless, research
Correct: C
S1: Mediterranean diet benefits heart health. S2: it also benefits brain health. Two parallel benefits being stacked = addition. "Furthermore" adds more of the same direction. A & D = contrast/concession — wrong. B = example — S2 isn't an example of S1; it's a separate category of benefit.
Q6Transitions — ConcessionHard
Critics of standardised testing argue that such assessments measure only a narrow range of academic skills and disadvantage students from lower socioeconomic backgrounds. Standardised tests provide a consistent benchmark that allows meaningful comparison of student achievement across schools, districts, and states.
Which transition, added to the beginning of the second sentence, best reflects the relationship?
  • A Similarly,
  • B Therefore,
  • C Even so,
  • D For instance,
Correct: C
S1: critics attack standardised tests. S2: defends them (consistent benchmark). Structure: here are the criticisms — Even so, there are genuine benefits. = concession. "Even so" = despite those valid criticisms, the tests still have value. A = similarity — wrong direction. B = result. D = example.
Q7Transitions — ExampleMedium
Many endangered species have made remarkable recoveries after targeted conservation interventions. The bald eagle population in the United States rose from fewer than 500 nesting pairs in the 1960s to over 9,000 pairs today following the banning of DDT and the protection of nesting habitats.
Which transition best connects the two sentences?
  • A However, the bald eagle
  • B As a result, the bald eagle
  • C For instance, the bald eagle
  • D Nevertheless, the bald eagle
Correct: C
S1: general claim — species can recover. S2: specific case of the bald eagle illustrating that claim. Specific case supporting a general claim = example. "For instance" is correct. A & D = contrast/concession. B = result — the bald eagle recovery didn't result from the general claim.
Q8Transitions — ConcessionHard
Nuclear energy produces virtually no greenhouse gas emissions during operation, making it one of the lowest-carbon electricity sources available. The unsolved challenge of safely storing radioactive waste for thousands of years poses a profound long-term risk that no country has yet fully resolved.
Which transition, inserted at the beginning of the second sentence, best reflects the relationship?
  • A Furthermore,
  • B As a result,
  • C Nevertheless,
  • D For example,
Correct: C
S1: nuclear = low carbon emissions (benefit). S2: radioactive waste = major problem (drawback). Structure: nuclear has a real benefit — Nevertheless, it has a serious unresolved problem. Concession — accepting the benefit before pivoting to the limitation. A = addition — same direction. B = result. D = example.
Q9Transitions — Cause/ResultMedium
Excessive screen time before bed suppresses the production of melatonin, the hormone that regulates the sleep-wake cycle. Young adults who use devices in the hour before sleeping consistently report longer sleep-onset times and poorer sleep quality than those who do not.
Which transition best connects the two sentences?
  • A In contrast, young adults
  • B For instance, young adults
  • C However, young adults

Correct: D
S1: screens suppress melatonin (cause). S2: young adults who use devices sleep worse (result/evidence of the cause). "As a result" signals the consequence of melatonin suppression. A & C = contrast. B = example — S2 isn't illustrating a general claim; it's the direct outcome of S1.
Q10Transitions — ContrastHard
Traditional paper maps require no battery power, function in areas with no signal, and are not vulnerable to software failures or cyberattacks. GPS navigation systems have largely displaced paper maps in everyday use, and many younger drivers report being unable to read a traditional map at all.

S1: paper maps have clear advantages. S2: GPS has replaced them anyway. Unexpected outcome given S1 — despite the advantages, GPS won. "Despite these advantages" captures the concession/contrast perfectly. A = result — GPS displacement didn't result from paper maps' advantages. B = addition. D = example.

• 1 in 3 American adults regularly sleeps fewer than 7 hours
• Sleep deprivation linked to increased risk of obesity, diabetes, and cardiovascular disease
• Impaired sleep reduces immune function and increases susceptibility to infection
• Estimated economic cost of sleep deprivation in the US: $411 billion per year
The student wants to emphasise both the scale of the problem and its economic impact. Which choice best accomplishes this?
  • A Sleep deprivation is linked to serious health risks including obesity, diabetes, and reduced immune function.
  • B The economic cost of sleep deprivation in the US is estimated at $411 billion annually, reflecting the widespread nature of the condition.
  • C With one in three American adults regularly failing to get adequate sleep, the resulting health consequences and lost productivity cost the US economy an estimated $411 billion each year.
  • D Sleep deprivation affects millions of Americans and is associated with a range of serious medical conditions.

Goal: BOTH scale AND economic impact. C covers both: scale (1 in 3 adults) and economic cost ($411 billion). B mentions the cost but weakly implies scale. A = health risks only. D = scale only ("millions") but no economic figure.
Q12Rhetorical SynthesisMedium

• Uses up to 95% less water than conventional farming
• Produces up to 100× more food per square metre than traditional agriculture
• Currently more expensive to operate than conventional farming due to energy costs
• Energy costs may fall as renewable energy becomes cheaper
The student wants to present vertical farming as a promising but currently limited solution. Which choice best accomplishes this goal?
  • A Vertical farming uses 95% less water and produces far more food per square metre than conventional agriculture, representing a significant technological advance.
  • B Vertical farming is currently too expensive to compete with conventional farming due to high energy costs.

Q13Rhetorical SynthesisHard
Notes:
• The placebo effect: patients improve after receiving an inert treatment they believe is real
• Placebo effects have been documented in pain, depression, Parkinson's disease, and irritable bowel syndrome
• Open-label placebos (patients told they are receiving a placebo) still produce measurable improvements
• Placebo effects involve real neurological changes, including release of endorphins and dopamine
• Effect size varies by condition, delivery context, and patient expectations
The student wants to argue that the placebo effect is a genuine physiological phenomenon, not merely psychological self-deception. Which choice best accomplishes this goal?
  • A The placebo effect has been documented across a wide range of conditions, from pain and depression to Parkinson's disease.
  • B Because patients improve even when they know they are receiving a placebo, the effect cannot be explained by belief or expectation alone.
  • C Far from being mere self-deception, the placebo effect produces real neurological changes — including the release of endorphins and dopamine — and persists even when patients are told they are receiving an inert treatment.
  • D The placebo effect varies significantly by condition and context, suggesting that patient expectations play a key role in determining its strength.
Correct: C
Goal: argue placebo = genuine physiological phenomenon, NOT just psychological. C does both: directly rebuts "self-deception" framing, cites real neurological changes (endorphins, dopamine), and adds open-label evidence. A = breadth of conditions — doesn't argue physiological. B = open-label evidence — argues against expectation but doesn't establish physiological mechanism. D = variation/context — actually undermines the physiological argument.
Q14Rhetorical SynthesisHard
Notes:
• Antibiotic resistance: bacteria evolve to survive antibiotic treatment
• Over 700,000 deaths per year globally attributed to antibiotic-resistant infections
• By 2050, resistance could cause 10 million deaths annually if unchecked
• Overuse in livestock farming accounts for approximately 70% of global antibiotic consumption
• New antibiotic development has slowed dramatically since the 1980s
The student wants to convey the urgency of the antibiotic resistance crisis using projected future harm alongside a root cause. Which choice best accomplishes this?
  • A Antibiotic resistance already kills over 700,000 people annually, and the pharmaceutical industry's declining investment in new antibiotic development has worsened the situation.
  • B Antibiotic resistance is driven largely by overuse in livestock farming, which accounts for roughly 70% of global antibiotic consumption.
  • C Unless action is taken to curb antibiotic overuse — particularly in livestock farming, which drives roughly 70% of global consumption — resistance could kill 10 million people annually by 2050.
  • D With new antibiotic development slowing and resistance rising, the medical community faces a narrowing window to address a crisis that currently kills 700,000 people per year.
Correct: C
Goal: URGENCY + projected FUTURE HARM + root CAUSE. C: future harm (10 million by 2050), root cause (livestock farming, 70%), and urgency framing ("unless action is taken"). A = current deaths + pharma slowdown — no future projection, not the stated root cause. B = root cause only. D = urgency + current deaths + development slowdown — no future projection figure.

• Finnish students begin formal schooling at age 7, two years later than most countries

• Teachers in Finland are selected from the top third of university graduates
• Finnish students have among the least homework of any high-performing country
The student wants to challenge the assumption that more instruction time and testing leads to better educational outcomes. Which choice best accomplishes this goal using the notes?
  • A Finland's teachers are drawn from the top third of university graduates, suggesting that teacher quality may matter more than instructional time.
  • B Finland consistently outperforms most nations in global education rankings despite its teachers being among the most highly trained in the world.

D = homework only — one data point, doesn't mention testing.
Q16Rhetorical SynthesisHard

Should the underlined sentence be kept or deleted?

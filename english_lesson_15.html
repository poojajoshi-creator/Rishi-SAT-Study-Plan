What are sentence boundaries?
Step 1
The core rule

An independent clause is a complete sentence — it has a subject and a verb and can stand alone. Two independent clauses must be joined correctly. The SAT tests three wrong ways and four right ways.

3 wrong ways (always errors):
1. Comma splice: "She studied hard, she passed the exam." — comma alone is never enough
2. Run-on / fused sentence: "She studied hard she passed the exam." — nothing between them
3. Wrong connector: "She studied hard, however she passed." — "however" is an adverb, not a conjunction

4 correct ways:
1. Period: "She studied hard. She passed."
2. Semicolon: "She studied hard; she passed."
3. Comma + FANBOYS: "She studied hard, so she passed." (For / And / Nor / But / Or / Yet / So)
4. Subordinating conjunction: "Because she studied hard, she passed." — makes one clause dependent

The independent clause test

Before choosing punctuation, test each side of the blank: can this part stand alone as a complete sentence?

If BOTH sides = complete sentences → you need one of the 4 correct joiners.
If only ONE side = complete sentence → a comma alone is often fine (e.g. after an introductory phrase).
If NEITHER side = complete sentence → the sentence has a different problem.

⭐
Strategy
Step 2
1
Identify what's being tested. Look at how the four choices differ — punctuation only? A conjunction added or removed? This tells you exactly what rule to apply.
2
Test each side of the blank. Read the left side: complete sentence? Read the right side: complete sentence? If both yes — you need one of the 4 correct joiners.
3
Apply the rule, don't trust your ear. "The results were surprising, however the team continued" sounds almost right — but it's a comma splice. The rule overrides how it sounds.
⚠️
Traps
Step 3
Trap 1
Transitional adverbs as conjunctions
However, therefore, furthermore, consequently, moreover — these are ADVERBS, not conjunctions. "Clause, however, clause" is a comma splice. You need a semicolon: "Clause; however, clause."
"The data was clear, however, the team disagreed." ✗ → "The data was clear; however, the team disagreed." ✓
Trap 2
Comma + FANBOYS only
You must have BOTH a comma AND a FANBOYS conjunction. "She studied hard so she passed" (no comma) is technically a fused sentence for formal writing. The SAT wants the comma.
"She left early so she caught the train." ✗ → "She left early, so she caught the train." ✓
Trap 3
Subordinating conjunctions
Because, although, since, while, when, if, unless — these make the clause DEPENDENT. "Because she studied" is not a complete sentence. Correct: "Because she studied, she passed." The dependent clause needs the independent one.
Trap 4
Semicolon ≠ colon
Semicolons join two complete sentences (= period). Colons introduce a list, explanation, or elaboration after a complete sentence — what follows a colon does NOT need to be a complete sentence.

Worked examples
Step 4
Example 1 — Comma splice with transitional adverbMedium
The new bridge design reduced construction costs by 18%, _______ it also cut the projected completion time by six months.
Which choice correctly joins the two clauses?
  • A. costs by 18%, additionally it
  • B. costs by 18% it
  • C. costs by 18%; additionally, it
  • D. costs by 18%, and additionally it
Answer: C. Both sides are complete sentences. "Additionally" is a transitional adverb — it cannot join clauses alone with just a comma (A = comma splice). B = run-on. D = comma + "and additionally" is redundant and awkward. C = semicolon correctly joins two independent clauses; "additionally" then functions as an adverb modifying the second clause.
Example 2 — Choosing the right conjunctionMedium
The researchers expected the experiment to fail _______ the results exceeded every benchmark they had set.
Which choice correctly completes the sentence?
  • A. fail, however
  • B. fail
  • C. fail, yet
  • D. fail; the results
Answer: C. Both sides = complete sentences. A (comma + however) = comma splice — "however" is an adverb. B = run-on. C = comma + "yet" (FANBOYS) = correct. D = semicolon correct structurally but the original has "the results" already — no word needed after the semicolon fits the blank.
Example 3 — Subordinating conjunctionHard
_______ the population of the town had declined significantly over the previous decade, the local school board voted to close two of its four elementary schools.
Which choice correctly begins the sentence?
  • A. The population of the town had declined significantly over the previous decade,
  • B. The population of the town had declined significantly over the previous decade;
  • C. Because the population of the town had declined significantly over the previous decade,
  • D. The population of the town had declined significantly over the previous decade, and
Answer: C. The sentence has a causal relationship — population decline caused the school closures. "Because" creates a dependent clause that logically connects to the independent clause "the school board voted..." A = comma splice (two independent clauses joined by comma alone). B = semicolon then the second half isn't a new sentence — broken structure. D = "and" implies addition, not causation. C correctly uses "because" to show causation and the comma separates the dependent from independent clause.

Practice — 5 questions
Step 5
Practice 1
The documentary received widespread critical acclaim _______ it failed to attract a mainstream audience, earning less than $2 million at the box office.
Which choice correctly joins the clauses?
  • A. acclaim, however
  • B. acclaim, but
  • C. acclaim; but
  • D. acclaim it
B. Comma + "but" (FANBOYS) correctly joins two independent clauses showing contrast. A = comma splice ("however" is an adverb). C = semicolon + "but" — never use a coordinating conjunction after a semicolon. D = run-on.
Practice 2
The medication reduces inflammation effectively _______ patients must be monitored for potential side effects including elevated blood pressure.
Which choice is correct?
  • A. effectively, therefore
  • B. effectively; however, patients
  • C. effectively, patients
  • D. effectively patients
B. Two independent clauses with a contrasting relationship. Semicolon + "however" + comma is the correct structure for a transitional adverb joining independent clauses. A = comma splice (therefore is an adverb). C = comma splice (no conjunction). D = run-on.
Practice 3
_______ the bridge had been structurally compromised for years, city engineers did not recommend closure until the most recent inspection revealed critical deterioration.
Which choice correctly begins the sentence?
  • A. The bridge had been structurally compromised for years,
  • B. Although the bridge had been structurally compromised for years,
  • C. The bridge had been structurally compromised for years;
  • D. The bridge had been structurally compromised for years and
B. "Although" creates a dependent clause showing concession — the bridge was compromised BUT engineers didn't act until recently. "Although" + comma before the independent clause is correct. A = comma splice. C = semicolon leaves the second part without a proper connection. D = "and" implies addition, not concession.
Practice 4
The team's star player was injured in the second quarter _______ the reserves performed admirably, securing a narrow victory.
Which choice is correct?
  • A. quarter, yet
  • B. quarter nevertheless
  • C. quarter, nevertheless
  • D. quarter; yet
A. Comma + "yet" (FANBOYS) is correct. B = run-on (no punctuation before adverb). C = comma + "nevertheless" = comma splice ("nevertheless" is an adverb, not a conjunction). D = semicolon + "yet" — don't combine semicolon with a coordinating conjunction.
Practice 5 — Hard
The philosopher argued that consciousness cannot be fully explained by physical processes alone _______ this claim, known as the "hard problem," remains one of the most contested positions in contemporary philosophy of mind.
Which choice correctly punctuates the sentence?
  • A. alone, this
  • B. alone and this
  • C. alone; this
  • D. alone, and this
C. Both sides are independent clauses. No conjunction is needed here — the semicolon joins them cleanly. A = comma splice. B = "and" without comma = fused sentence for formal writing. D = comma + "and" works grammatically but creates an awkward meaning — the second clause isn't being added, it's a separate related observation. C (semicolon) is cleaner and correct.

Summary card
Keep this
Sentence Boundaries — Everything you need
4 correct ways to join
Period · Semicolon · Comma + FANBOYS (For And Nor But Or Yet So) · Subordinating conjunction (because, although, since, while, when, if)
3 errors
Comma splice (comma alone) · Run-on (nothing) · Comma + transitional adverb (however, therefore, furthermore = NOT conjunctions)
The test
Can each side stand alone as a complete sentence? If both yes → need one of the 4 correct joiners.
Critical rule
However / therefore / furthermore / consequently / moreover = adverbs. With semicolon: "Clause; however, clause." ✓. With comma alone: ALWAYS wrong.
Semicolon rule
Semicolon = period. Complete sentence on BOTH sides. Never "semicolon + but/and/or" — that doubles up.
Subordinating conjunctions
Because / although / since / while / when / if → make one clause dependent. Dependent clause + comma + independent clause = correct structure.

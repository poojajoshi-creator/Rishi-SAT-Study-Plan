Where the points are — your four focus areas
The key insight: these four areas together account for the entire +220 point gap. Fix them, and 1500 follows. You don't need to be perfect everywhere — you need to be excellent in these four places.
Priority 1 · Biggest lever
Standard English Conventions
490 → 680+
SEC covers punctuation (commas, semicolons, colons, dashes, apostrophes) and grammar (subject-verb agreement, modifiers, parallelism, pronoun agreement). These are the most rule-based questions on the test — no interpretation needed. Once the rules are memorised and automatic, they become the fastest points to earn. Getting from 490 to 680 here alone is worth ~40 RW points.
Priority 2 · Reading core
Information & Ideas
550 → 650+
This covers inference questions, evidence-for-claims, words in context, main idea, and cross-text connections. The issue isn't reading ability — it's a systematic approach to each question type. Once you learn what each question is actually asking for and how to eliminate wrong answers, these get much more reliable.
Priority 3 · Math reading
Problem-Solving & Data Analysis
550 → 680+
Probability, two-way tables, mean/median/mode, percentage change, standard deviation, line of best fit, sampling. These questions are more about careful reading and applying formulas than deep mathematical ability. A focused 2-week module will cover all the question types that appear on the SAT.
Priority 4 · Algebra push
Advanced Math
610 → 700+
Quadratics, rational equations, polynomial operations, function composition, and exponential models. You're already solid here — this is about eliminating the 2–3 questions per module that are currently slipping. A targeted drill on the specific question types you're missing will push this band up.

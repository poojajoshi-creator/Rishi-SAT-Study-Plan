Concept 27 / 27SEC — Grammar
Concise &
Redundant Language
The SAT always prefers the shortest option that preserves the full meaning. If two choices say the same thing, the shorter one is correct. Redundancy, wordiness, and unnecessary repetition are always wrong.
~2–3 per moduleTarget: 25 secRule: shortest that keeps the meaning = correct
✂
What the SAT tests
Step 1
Three types of wordiness

Type 1 — Redundancy (saying the same thing twice):
✗ "future plans" (plans are always future) · "past history" (history is always past)
✗ "end result" · "final conclusion" · "completely destroyed" · "brief summary"
✗ "She nodded her head in agreement." → "She nodded in agreement." (nodding = head movement)
✗ "The reason why is because..." → "The reason is that..." or "because..."

Type 2 — Circumlocution (taking many words to say something a few words can):
✗ "due to the fact that" → "because"
✗ "in spite of the fact that" → "although"
✗ "at this point in time" → "now"
✗ "in the event that" → "if"
✗ "has the ability to" → "can"

Type 3 — Unnecessary context already stated:
If the sentence already tells us something, the answer choice that repeats that information is wrong.
"The nineteenth-century poet, who lived in the 1800s, wrote in a distinctive style." — "who lived in the 1800s" repeats "nineteenth-century."

⭐
Strategy
Step 2
1
Compare the choices. What's different between them? Usually one adds words, one removes them. Ask whether the extra words add any new meaning.
2
Eliminate anything that repeats information already in the sentence. If the passage already says X, an answer choice that also says X in different words is redundant — eliminate it.
3
Pick the shortest choice that preserves the full meaning. "Shortest" doesn't mean "most incomplete" — it means no unnecessary words. Verify nothing essential is lost.
4
Watch for "OMIT" as an answer choice. On questions asking what to do with an underlined portion, "omit the underlined portion" is often the correct answer when the underlined text is redundant.
⚠️
Traps
Step 3
Trap 1
Longer feels more complete
Students pick longer answers because they feel safer and more thorough. But on conciseness questions, the longer choice is almost always wrong. Extra words = wrong unless they add genuinely new meaning.
Trap 2
Cutting too much
The shortest choice removes something essential. Check: does the shortest version still make logical sense and preserve the full intended meaning? If not, it's gone too far.
Trap 3
Redundancy hidden in context
The redundancy isn't in the answer itself but between the answer and the surrounding sentence. Read the whole sentence to check whether the answer choice repeats something already established earlier.
Trap 4
"The reason is because"
"The reason the experiment failed is because of insufficient funding." Wrong — "because" gives a reason and so does "the reason is." Correct: "The reason the experiment failed was insufficient funding." or "The experiment failed because of insufficient funding."

Worked examples
Step 4
Example 1 — Context redundancyMedium
Marie Curie, the pioneering physicist and chemist who conducted groundbreaking research on radioactivity, was a scientist who made important contributions to the field of science and conducted research that was considered groundbreaking at the time.
  • A. No change
  • B. was a pioneering figure in the sciences
  • C. became the first woman to win a Nobel Prize
  • D. is widely regarded as one of the most important scientists of the twentieth century
C. The opening already tells us she was a "pioneering physicist and chemist who conducted groundbreaking research." A, B, and D all repeat this information in different words — redundant. C adds genuinely new information (first woman to win a Nobel Prize) that isn't stated anywhere in the sentence. New meaning = not redundant = correct.
Example 2 — CircumlocutionEasy
The project was delayed due to the fact that the primary contractor failed to secure the necessary permits.
  • A. No change
  • B. because
  • C. owing to the reason that
  • D. in light of the fact that
B. "Due to the fact that" = 5 words meaning "because" = 1 word. All four choices mean the same thing — "because" is the most concise. C and D are even wordier than A. On the SAT, when all choices have the same meaning, the shortest is always correct.
Example 3 — Hidden redundancyHard
The annual report, published every year, showed that revenue had increased by 12% in the third quarter.
  • A. No change
  • B. Remove "published every year"
  • C. Replace with "published on an annual basis"
  • D. Replace with "which comes out yearly"
B. "Annual" already means "every year." "Published every year" repeats "annual" — redundant. Remove it entirely: "The annual report showed that revenue had increased by 12%." A, C, and D all keep the redundancy in different forms.

Practice — 5 questions
Step 5
Practice 1
The novel's plot, which was described as complex and intricate by reviewers, had a storyline that many readers found to be difficult to follow due to its complicated and hard-to-understand nature.
  • A. No change
  • B. was something that many readers struggled to understand
  • C. confused many readers
  • D. presented readers with a complex and intricate storyline that proved challenging
C. The opening already tells us the plot is "complex and intricate." A repeats "complicated and hard-to-understand" — redundant. B is shorter but still wordy ("something that many readers struggled to understand"). D repeats "complex and intricate" from the opening. C adds the essential new information (readers were confused) in just three words with no repetition.
Practice 2
The committee will make a final decision at some point in the future when it has had enough time to consider all the relevant factors and evidence that have been presented to it.
  • A. No change
  • B. after it has considered all the relevant factors
  • C. after further deliberation
  • D. once it has reviewed the evidence and factors presented
C. "At some point in the future when it has had enough time to consider all the relevant factors and evidence that have been presented to it" = 29 words. "After further deliberation" = 3 words. Same meaning — the committee will decide after thinking things through. C is the most concise correct answer. B and D are shorter than A but still wordier than C.
Practice 3
The reason the experiment produced unexpected results is because the temperature fluctuated beyond the acceptable range.
  • A. No change
  • B. was that
  • C. is due to the fact that
  • D. is because of the fact that
B. "The reason...is because" is redundant — "because" gives a reason and "the reason is" already signals a reason. Correct construction: "The reason...was that [cause]." B fixes this. C and D are even wordier versions of the same error. A is the original error.
Practice 4
The spacecraft, launched into space in 2021, has the capability of travelling at speeds in excess of 28,000 kilometres per hour.
  • A. No change
  • B. can travel at speeds exceeding 28,000 kilometres per hour
  • C. is capable of achieving speeds that exceed 28,000 kilometres per hour
  • D. travels at speeds that are in excess of 28,000 kilometres per hour
B. "Has the capability of travelling" = "can travel." All choices convey the same meaning. B is the shortest and most direct. A (has the capability of travelling) = 5 words for "can travel" = 2 words. C and D are also wordy. "Can" always beats "has the ability/capability to" on the SAT.
Practice 5 — Hard (context redundancy)
In 1969, astronauts Neil Armstrong and Buzz Aldrin became the first humans to walk on the surface of the Moon during the Apollo 11 mission. This historic Moon landing, which took place in 1969, marked a landmark achievement in the history of human space exploration.
  • A. No change
  • B. The Moon landing was a significant and historic achievement.
  • C. Omit the underlined sentence entirely.
  • D. Armstrong and Aldrin's achievement was widely celebrated around the world.
C. The previous sentence already told us: (1) it happened in 1969, (2) Armstrong and Aldrin walked on the Moon, (3) it was the Apollo 11 mission. The underlined sentence repeats "1969," "Moon landing," and "historic achievement" — all redundant. B still repeats "historic achievement." D adds slightly new info (celebrated worldwide) but the question asks about the full underlined sentence — the cleanest answer is to omit it entirely. When a sentence is entirely redundant, "omit" is always the correct choice.

Final lesson
Concise & Redundant Language — Everything you need
The rule
Shortest choice that preserves the full meaning = correct. Extra words add nothing → wrong. Missing essential meaning → too short.
Redundancy
Same idea expressed twice: "future plans" · "past history" · "annual...every year" · "the reason is because." Remove the repetition.
Circumlocution
"Due to the fact that" → because · "has the ability to" → can · "at this point in time" → now · "in the event that" → if
Context redundancy
Read the surrounding text. If an answer choice repeats something already stated in the sentence or paragraph → eliminate it.
"Omit" option
When an entire underlined sentence repeats what was just said → omit is correct. Always check if the passage already contains that information.
"The reason is because"
Always wrong. Fix: "The reason is that [noun]" OR simply "because [clause]." Never both "reason" and "because" together.

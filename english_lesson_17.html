Concept 17 / 27SEC — Punctuation
Colons
A colon is a promise: it says "what follows is the explanation, list, or elaboration of what I just said." The rule is simple — complete sentence before, anything after.
~1–2 per moduleTarget: 30 secRule: complete sentence MUST come before
:
What colons do
Step 1
The one rule

A colon must be preceded by an independent clause (a complete sentence). What comes after the colon can be anything — a word, a phrase, a list, another sentence, a quotation.

✓ "She brought three things to the meeting: her laptop, her notes, and her coffee."
✓ "The experiment produced a surprising result: the control group outperformed the test group."
✗ "She brought: her laptop, her notes, and her coffee." — "She brought" is not a complete sentence without the object.

Test: Remove the colon and everything after it. Does what's left make a complete sentence? If yes → colon is correct. If no → colon is wrong.

Colon vs semicolon

Semicolon = two complete sentences, equal relationship. "She studied hard; she passed."
Colon = complete sentence, then explanation/list/elaboration. "She passed for one reason: she studied every night."
Key: what follows a colon does NOT need to be a complete sentence. What follows a semicolon MUST be a complete sentence.

⭐
Strategy
Step 2
1
Cover the colon and everything after it. Read what's left. Is it a complete sentence (subject + verb)? If yes → colon is valid. If no → colon is wrong.
2
Check the relationship. Does what follows explain, list, or elaborate on the first part? If yes → colon is the right choice over a semicolon.
3
Never put a colon between a verb and its object or between a preposition and its object. "The study found: three significant results" is wrong — "found" needs its object directly.
⚠️
Traps
Step 3
Trap 1
Colon after a fragment
"The report highlighted three areas: ..." — "The report highlighted" without "three areas" is complete. But "The most important issues were:" is a fragment (no predicate complement). Check both sides.
Trap 2
Colon between verb and object
"The scientists discovered: a new species..." — wrong. "Discovered" needs its object immediately. A colon interrupts this natural grammatical connection.
Trap 3
Colon when semicolon is needed
Two independent clauses with no explanatory relationship → semicolon, not colon. "She studied all night: she was well prepared" — the second clause explains the first, so colon is fine. "She studied: her brother watched TV" — no explanatory relationship, colon is wrong.

Worked examples
Step 4
Example 1Easy
The chef prepared several regional specialities for the banquet _______ lamb tagine, a saffron-infused rice pilaf, and a honey-almond pastry.
Which punctuation correctly completes the sentence?
  • A. banquet,
  • B. banquet:
  • C. banquet;
  • D. banquet —
B. "The chef prepared several regional specialities for the banquet" is a complete sentence. What follows is the list of those specialities — the colon introduces the elaboration perfectly. A (comma) is too weak for a list following a complete sentence. C (semicolon) requires a complete sentence after it — "lamb tagine, a saffron-infused rice pilaf..." is not a sentence. D (dash) works but colon is more formal and precise for lists.
Example 2 — Fragment before colonMedium
The committee's main recommendation was: that all government agencies adopt a unified cybersecurity framework by the end of the fiscal year.
Which revision corrects the punctuation?
  • A. No change
  • B. Remove the colon — the sentence needs no punctuation between "was" and "that"
  • C. Replace the colon with a semicolon
  • D. Replace the colon with a comma
B. "The committee's main recommendation was" is not a complete sentence — "was" needs its complement to complete the meaning. The colon interrupts the verb from its complement clause "that all government agencies..." Remove the colon: "The committee's main recommendation was that all government agencies adopt..."
Example 3 — Colon vs semicolonHard
For decades, astronomers searched for evidence of water on Mars _______ in 2018, ESA's MARSIS radar finally confirmed the presence of a subglacial lake beneath the southern polar ice cap.
Which punctuation best completes the sentence?
  • A. Mars,
  • B. Mars, and
  • C. Mars;
  • D. Mars:
C. Both sides are independent clauses. The second clause describes what happened AFTER the search — not an explanation OF the search, but a sequel to it. Semicolon joins two related independent clauses. D (colon) — the discovery doesn't explain the searching; it concludes it. The relationship is sequential, not explanatory, making a semicolon more appropriate. A = comma splice. B = comma + "and" works but is less precise than semicolon here.

Practice — 5 questions
Step 5
Practice 1
The novel's central theme is clear from the opening chapter _______ every human relationship is defined by power.
  • A. chapter,
  • B. chapter and
  • C. chapter:
  • D. chapter;
C. "The novel's central theme is clear from the opening chapter" = complete sentence. "Every human relationship is defined by power" = the theme being explained. Colon introduces what the theme IS — explanatory relationship. Semicolon (D) would work structurally but is less precise than a colon for an explanation.
Practice 2
The new regulation requires: all manufacturers to disclose the full chemical composition of their products on the label.
  • A. No change
  • B. Remove the colon
  • C. Replace the colon with a comma
  • D. Replace the colon with a semicolon
B. "Requires" needs its object directly — "all manufacturers to disclose..." is the object. Inserting a colon between a verb and its object breaks the grammatical connection. Remove it completely.
Practice 3
Scientists have identified three factors that most strongly predict volcanic eruption _______ increased seismic activity, ground deformation, and changes in gas emissions.
  • A. eruption,
  • B. eruption and
  • C. eruption:
  • D. eruption;
C. "Scientists have identified three factors that most strongly predict volcanic eruption" = complete sentence. The list that follows IS those three factors — the colon introduces the elaboration. D (semicolon) is wrong — the list is not a complete sentence.
Practice 4
The archaeologist made a discovery that would rewrite the region's history _______ a burial chamber containing artefacts dated to 3,500 BCE.
  • A. history,
  • B. history;
  • C. history:
  • D. history, and
C. Complete sentence before: "The archaeologist made a discovery that would rewrite the region's history." What follows: "a burial chamber containing artefacts dated to 3,500 BCE" — this IS the discovery (explanation/elaboration). Colon is perfect. B (semicolon) — the phrase after is not an independent clause.
Practice 5 — Hard
The reason for the experiment's failure was not a flaw in the methodology but something far more mundane _______ a graduate student had mislabelled the sample containers.
  • A. mundane,
  • B. mundane;
  • C. mundane:
  • D. mundane —
C. "Something far more mundane" is the subject's complement — and the colon delivers WHAT that something is. Complete sentence before (yes — "The reason...was...something far more mundane"). The second clause explains what "something far more mundane" is. Colon. D (dash) also works grammatically — but colon is more precise for formal explanatory elaboration.

Summary card
Keep this
Colons — Everything you need
The one rule
Complete sentence MUST come before the colon. Cover and test: does what's left stand alone? If not, no colon.
What follows
Anything: a word, phrase, list, sentence, quotation. What follows a colon does NOT need to be a complete sentence.
Colon vs semicolon
Colon = explanation/list follows. Semicolon = two complete sentences, equal relationship. Both need a complete sentence before them.
Never
Between verb and object · Between preposition and object · After "including," "such as," "for example"

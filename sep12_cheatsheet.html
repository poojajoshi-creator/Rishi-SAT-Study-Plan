SEC — Week 1
Punctuation Rules
1 · Sentence Boundaries
Two ICs joined by comma alone = comma splice ❌. Fix with: period / semicolon / comma+FANBOYS. "However/therefore/nevertheless" are NOT conjunctions — need ; [adverb], before the next clause.
2 · Semicolons
Both sides must be independent clauses. Never semicolon before a list or a phrase. With transitional adverbs: [IC]; however, [IC]
3 · Colons
Left side = complete IC ✓. Right side = anything. Never after "including," "such as," or "for example." Test: can the left stand alone? Yes = colon OK.
4 · Paired Dashes
Opening dash → must close with a dash. Never dash open + comma/semicolon close. Remove parenthetical → sentence must still work.
5 · Commas — 4 legal uses only
Comma+FANBOYS (join 2 ICs) · After intro element · Around non-essential info (who/which, removable) · In lists. No comma between subject and verb. No comma before "that."
6 · Apostrophes
it's = it is · its = possessive. who's = who is · whose = possessive. Never apostrophe for a plain plural. Plural possessive: scientists' (after s). Singular: scientist's (before s).
SEC — Week 2
Grammar Rules
1 · Subject-Verb
Strip phrases → match verb to real subject. Neither/nor → closer subject wins. Collective nouns (team, jury, committee) → singular. Each/every/either → singular.
2 · Verb Tense
Stay consistent unless a genuine time shift. Look for time signals (then/now/currently). Don't switch tense mid-list.
3 · Pronoun
Match antecedent in number. Collective nouns → it/its not they/their. Vague "this/it/they" = wrong → replace with the specific noun.
4 · Modifiers
Opening phrase doer = subject. Possessive noun trap: "the scientist's work" ≠ the scientist. Test: does the phrase logically describe the subject?
5 · Parallelism
Lists: all same form (gerund / infinitive / noun). Not only X but also Y → both sides must be parallel. Compare like to like: results vs. those of.
6 · Conciseness
Shortest correct answer wins. "Reason... is because" → redundant. "Due to the fact that" → because. "In order to" → to. "End result" → result.
Reading & Writing
Info & Ideas
Inference / Main Idea
State author's point in your own words first. Eliminate: too extreme / too far / contradicts / too vague. Right answer = modest + traceable to the text.
Evidence for Claims
Underline the exact claim. Test each answer: does it support that specific claim? "Right topic, wrong claim" = most common wrong answer.
Words in Context
Cover the word. Predict meaning from context. Never pick the common dictionary definition — the SAT always tests secondary meanings. Substitute and check.
Cross-Text
One sentence per text: "Text 1 says... Text 2 says..." Agree / disagree / qualify? Right answer = traceable to both texts.

Exam Day
Mindset Reminders
What you've already got
Expression of Ideas: 680–800. Algebra: 680–800. Geometry: 680–800. These are real. Don't overthink them — bank the points fast and move on.
On SEC questions
Apply the rule. Don't go by feel or how it "sounds." Two ICs need a semicolon or comma+FANBOYS. Paired dash needs a closing dash. "its" has no apostrophe.
On reading questions
State the passage's point in your own words before looking at answers. Eliminate the three wrong ones — too extreme, too far, contradicts, too vague. Modest + traceable = right.
On data questions
Slow down. Identify the denominator before calculating. Read exactly what the question asks. Correlation ≠ causation — eliminate those answers immediately.
If stuck on a question
Best guess and move on. Every question is worth exactly 1 point. Never let one hard question cost you the time for three easy ones. Flag it and come back if time allows.
The week paid off
Three full practice tests. 20+ hours of focused prep. The rules are in your head. Trust the preparation — it's already done.

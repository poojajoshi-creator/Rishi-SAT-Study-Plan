Transitions
Cause & Result
Sentence A describes a cause, condition, or reason. Sentence B describes the outcome, consequence, or conclusion that follows from it.
~1–2 per moduleTarget: 30 secTest: can you put "so" between them?
⚡
Cause & Result transitions
Step 1
The words and their direction

From cause to result: therefore · thus · consequently · as a result · hence · accordingly · for this reason · this means that

From result back to cause: because · since · as · given that · due to

The test: Can you replace the blank with "so"? If yes → result. Can you replace with "because"? If yes → cause.

Critical distinction from contrast: In a cause/result relationship, Sentence B FOLLOWS FROM Sentence A. In contrast, Sentence B OPPOSES Sentence A.

The mechanism test

Ask: "Did Sentence A make Sentence B happen, or contribute to it?" If yes → cause/result. If not → look for contrast or addition.

Example: "The temperature dropped below freezing. As a result, the pipes burst." The cold temperature CAUSED the pipes to burst.

False cause example: "The temperature dropped. However, the forecast had predicted mild weather." No causal link — this is contrast.

⭐
Strategy
Step 2
1
Name the relationship first. Ask: same direction (and/furthermore), opposite (but/however), or does A cause B (so/therefore)?
2
Apply the "so" test. Read S1 [blank] S2. Try inserting "so." Does it make logical sense? If yes → result word.
3
Watch for correlation vs causation. Two events happening together don't mean one caused the other. The sentence structure tells you which relationship is intended.
⚠️
Traps
Step 3
Trap 1
"Consequently" for contrast
When the second sentence seems surprising, "consequently" can feel right. But surprising results are still results — only use it if A actually causes B.
Trap 2
"Therefore" for addition
"Therefore" sounds conclusive and formal. Students pick it when they want the second sentence to feel important — but it only works if A logically produces B.

Worked examples
Step 4
Example 1Easy
The river flooded its banks after three consecutive days of heavy rainfall. _______, hundreds of residents in the low-lying areas were forced to evacuate their homes.
Which transition best completes the text?
  • A. However
  • B. As a result
  • C. In addition
  • D. For instance
B. Flooding → evacuation. The flood caused the evacuation. "As a result" = result follows cause.
Example 2 — Distinguishing result from contrastMedium
The antibiotic treatment was expected to clear the infection within five days. _______, the patient's fever persisted into the second week, suggesting the bacteria had developed resistance.
Which transition best completes the text?
  • A. Consequently
  • B. Therefore
  • C. However
  • D. As a result
C. Expected to clear → did NOT clear. The expectation was contradicted, not caused. This is contrast, not result. "Consequently/Therefore" would say the treatment PRODUCED the persistent fever — wrong.
Example 3 — HardHard
The study found a strong positive correlation between hours of sunlight exposure and reported mood ratings among participants. _______, the researchers were careful not to conclude that sunlight causes improved mood, noting that people who spend more time outdoors may already have lifestyles associated with better wellbeing.
Which transition best completes the text?
  • A. Therefore
  • B. Similarly
  • C. Nevertheless
  • D. Consequently
C. Strong correlation found → researchers still cautious about causation. The caution persists DESPITE the strong finding — concession/contrast. A and D would say the correlation CAUSED the caution — backwards. The caution is a methodological limitation independent of whether the correlation is strong.

Practice — 5 questions
Step 5
Practice 1
The company failed to renew its operating licence before the deadline. _______, all production at its three facilities was halted pending regulatory review.
Which transition best completes the text?
  • A. However
  • B. Consequently
  • C. Similarly
  • D. For example
B. Failed to renew licence → production halted. Direct legal consequence. "Consequently."
Practice 2
Voter turnout in the district had been declining for a decade. _______, community organisers launched a sustained registration drive that added 12,000 new voters to the rolls.
Which transition best completes the text?
  • A. As a result
  • B. Therefore
  • C. In response
  • D. Similarly
C. Declining turnout → organizers responded with registration drive. The drive is a REACTION to the decline, not a consequence of it. "In response" is a cause/result word that captures reactive causation. A and B would say the DECLINE caused the drive — close but "in response" is more precise for intentional reaction.
Practice 3
The new insulation reduced heat loss from the building by 40%. _______, the monthly heating bills fell significantly over the first winter.
Which transition best completes the text?
  • A. However
  • B. For instance
  • C. As a result
  • D. Nevertheless
C. Less heat loss → lower heating bills. Direct financial consequence of the insulation improvement.
Practice 4
The candidate's campaign raised $4 million in the final month. _______, she lost the election by a comfortable margin to her incumbent opponent.
Which transition best completes the text?
  • A. Consequently
  • B. As a result
  • C. Yet
  • D. For this reason
C. Raised $4M (positive signal) → lost election (unexpected result). Contrast — the fundraising didn't produce victory. A, B, D all imply fundraising caused the loss — wrong.
Practice 5
Deforestation eliminates the trees that absorb carbon dioxide from the atmosphere. _______, it is a major contributor to rising levels of atmospheric greenhouse gases.
Which transition best completes the text?
  • A. However
  • B. For example
  • C. Therefore
  • D. In contrast
C. Eliminating CO₂ absorbers → greenhouse gases rise. Deforestation directly causes the greenhouse effect. "Therefore" draws the logical conclusion.

Result words
therefore · thus · consequently · as a result · hence · accordingly · for this reason
The test
Replace blank with "so." If S1 [so] S2 makes sense → result. If S1 [but] S2 makes more sense → contrast.
Critical distinction
Result: B follows from A. Contrast: B opposes A. Surprising results are still results — only use contrast if B contradicts A.
Trap
"Therefore" for addition — "therefore" only works if A logically produces B. "Furthermore" for unexpected results — use "however."

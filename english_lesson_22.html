Pronoun Agreement
& Reference
A pronoun must agree with its antecedent in number and clearly refer to one specific noun. Vague or ambiguous pronouns are always wrong on the SAT.
~1–2 per moduleTarget: 35 secAsk: what does "it/they/their" refer to?
he→
Pronoun rules
Step 1
Rule 1 — Agreement

A pronoun must agree with its antecedent (the noun it replaces) in number:
Singular antecedent → singular pronoun: "The company released its annual report."
Plural antecedent → plural pronoun: "The companies released their annual reports."
Collective nouns → singular: "The team completed its season successfully."
Indefinite pronouns → singular: "Everyone must bring their own materials." (SAT now accepts "their" for singular indefinite — but "his or her" is always safe)

Rule 2 — Clear reference (no ambiguity)

Every pronoun must clearly refer to ONE specific antecedent. Ambiguous pronouns — those that could refer to more than one noun — are always errors.

✗ "When Maria met Sarah, she was nervous." — Who was nervous? Maria or Sarah?
✓ "When Maria met Sarah, Maria was nervous."

✗ "The government announced they would increase funding." — "they" has no clear antecedent (government is singular)
✓ "The government announced it would increase funding."

⭐
Strategy
Step 2
1
Find the pronoun and its antecedent. What noun does the pronoun replace? Write it out.
2
Check agreement. Is the antecedent singular or plural? Does the pronoun match? Collective nouns = singular → "it/its." Companies/researchers = plural → "they/their."
3
Check for ambiguity. Could the pronoun refer to more than one noun? If yes → the pronoun is wrong regardless of agreement. Replace with the noun itself.
⚠️
Traps
Step 3
Trap 1
"They" for singular collective nouns
"The board approved they're decision." Two errors: "they're" should be "their," and "the board" is singular — needs "its decision." "The board approved its decision."
Trap 2
Ambiguous "it" or "this"
"The study confirmed the hypothesis. It was remarkable." What was remarkable — the study or the hypothesis? Replace with the specific noun.
Trap 3
Pronoun before antecedent
"Before they published the findings, the researchers reviewed every data point." "They" refers to "the researchers" which comes after. This is acceptable in English — pronoun can precede the antecedent in complex sentences.

Worked examples
Step 4
Example 1 — Collective nounMedium
After months of deliberation, the committee announced their decision to restructure the organisation's funding model.
  • A. No change
  • B. its
  • C. the committee's
  • D. there
B. "Committee" = collective noun = singular. Singular pronoun → "its." A ("their") treats "committee" as plural. C (the committee's) is grammatically correct but unnecessarily wordy when "its" is clear. D ("there") is completely wrong — a location word.
Example 2 — Ambiguous pronounHard
When the new regulation was implemented alongside the existing policy, it caused significant confusion among businesses.
  • A. No change
  • B. the combination caused
  • C. they caused
  • D. this caused
B. "It" is ambiguous — does it refer to "the new regulation" or "the existing policy"? The confusion was caused by BOTH together ("alongside"). B clarifies by naming "the combination." A and D both leave the ambiguity. C (they) — two things are mentioned, but "they" is also ambiguous and adds a number error if one of the policies is the intended antecedent.

Practice — 5 questions
Step 5
Practice 1
The research team published their findings in a peer-reviewed journal after three years of fieldwork in the Amazon basin.
  • A. No change — "their" is correct
  • B. its findings
  • C. there findings
  • D. they're findings
A. "Research team" — while a collective noun, "team" here refers to the multiple researchers who collectively produced findings. "Their" is acceptable and natural here. B (its) is also grammatically possible for a collective noun, but "their" is standard when referring to the members' collective output in modern usage. C and D are wrong.
Practice 2
Each nation attending the summit must submit their climate pledge before the final session begins.
  • A. No change
  • B. its
  • C. there
  • D. they're
B. "Each nation" — "each" = singular. "Nation" = singular. Singular pronoun → "its." "Their" treats "each nation" as plural. The SAT tests this frequently: "each" makes everything that follows it singular.
Practice 3
The professor praised the student for her dissertation, saying it demonstrated an exceptional grasp of the field's most contested debates.
  • A. No change — "it" clearly refers to the dissertation
  • B. she
  • C. they
  • D. this
A. "It" clearly refers to "her dissertation" — the only singular neuter noun in the sentence. No ambiguity. "She demonstrated" (B) would mean the professor said the student herself demonstrated the grasp — awkward. "It" is correct and unambiguous here.
Practice 4
Analysts noted that the merger between the two firms would likely affect their combined market share, though the full impact remained unclear.
  • A. No change — "their" correctly refers to the two firms
  • B. its
  • C. the firms'
  • D. there
A. "Their" refers to "the two firms" — plural, so "their" is correct. B (its) would treat two firms as singular. C is wordy but not wrong — however A is more concise and correct. D (there) is a location word.
Practice 5 — Hard
When the city council reviewed the urban development plan alongside the environmental impact report, they concluded that additional green spaces were necessary.
  • A. No change
  • B. it concluded
  • C. the council concluded
  • D. the documents concluded
C. "They" is ambiguous — it could refer to "the city council" or to the two documents (the plan and the report). Only the council can "conclude" something — but the pronoun is unclear. C replaces the ambiguous "they" with "the council" to make it explicit. B (it) treats "council" as singular which is grammatically possible but ambiguous about which noun it refers to. D (the documents concluded) is wrong — documents don't conclude.

Pronoun Agreement & Reference — Everything you need
Agreement rule
Pronoun must match antecedent in number. Singular → it/its/he/she. Plural → they/their. Collective nouns → singular (its).
Clarity rule
Every pronoun must clearly refer to ONE noun. If "it" or "they" could refer to two nouns → ambiguous → wrong. Replace with the noun itself.
Each/every = singular
"Each company must update its records." Not "their." "Each" forces singular on everything that follows.
Collective nouns
team/committee/board/government/jury → "it/its." "The board made its decision." NOT "their decision."

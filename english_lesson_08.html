↔
What is a Contrast transition?
Step 1
Core definition

A contrast transition signals that the idea in Sentence B is different from, opposite to, or unexpected given the idea in Sentence A. The two sentences point in different directions.

Contrast words: however · nevertheless · yet · in contrast · on the other hand · whereas · while · unlike · conversely · instead · by contrast · that said

The test: Can you put "but" between the two ideas and have it make sense? If yes → contrast.

Contrast vs Concession — the critical distinction

Contrast sets up a direct opposition between two things or two situations:
"Dogs are loyal. In contrast, cats are independent." → Two different things compared.

Concession (Lesson 11) acknowledges a valid point before making a counter-argument:
"Dogs can be loyal. Nevertheless, they require significant daily care." → One thing, two aspects — the second aspect qualifies the first.

The SAT frequently puts both contrast and concession words as choices on the same question. The relationship determines which is correct — not how the sentence "sounds."

⭐
The 3-step method
Step 2
1
Read both sentences. Summarise each in one word or phrase: "Sentence A says [positive]." "Sentence B says [negative/opposite]."
2
Name the relationship. Contrast? Cause? Addition? Concession? Do this BEFORE looking at choices. If you can replace the blank with "but," it's contrast. If "so," it's cause/result. If "and," it's addition.
3
Match the relationship to the word. All contrast words are equivalent — pick the one the SAT offers that signals contrast. Never pick a word that sounds good without naming the relationship first.
⚠️
The traps
Step 3
Trap 1
Sounds right without classifying
"However" sounds smart and flexible. Students pick it by feel without checking the relationship. Always classify first — "however" is contrast/concession, not addition or cause.
Trap 2
Contrast vs Concession confusion
Both use "however" and "nevertheless." Contrast: two different things. Concession: one thing acknowledged before a qualifier. Check which structure the sentence uses.
Trap 3
Adding confusion words
"Furthermore" and "additionally" are addition words that sound formal and are often placed as distractors in contrast questions. If the second sentence contradicts the first, addition is always wrong.
Trap 4
Temporal words misread as contrast
"Subsequently" (then) and "meanwhile" (at the same time) are time words, not contrast words. If the sentences describe different time periods, you may need a time word, not a contrast word.

Worked examples
Step 4
Example 1 — Clear contrastEasy
Urban populations worldwide are growing rapidly as people migrate from rural areas in search of economic opportunities. _______, rural communities in many regions are experiencing significant population decline and the closure of local services.
💭 Thinking
S1: urban populations growing. S2: rural populations declining. Direct opposition between two different things (urban vs rural). → Contrast.
Which transition best completes the text?
  • A. Similarly
  • B. As a result
  • C. In contrast
  • D. Furthermore
Answer: C. Urban growing vs rural declining = direct contrast between two different things. "In contrast" names this precisely. A (Similarly) = addition/same direction — wrong. B (As a result) = cause/effect — urban growth doesn't cause rural decline here (correlation, not causation). D (Furthermore) = addition — wrong direction.
Example 2 — Unexpected resultMedium
The new traffic calming measures were implemented specifically to reduce vehicle speeds in the school zone. _______, average speeds recorded in the first month after installation were virtually unchanged from pre-intervention levels.
💭 Thinking
S1: measures intended to reduce speed. S2: speed unchanged. The second sentence describes the opposite of what S1 led us to expect. → Contrast.
Which transition best completes the text?
  • A. Consequently
  • B. In addition
  • C. However
  • D. Therefore
Answer: C. Intended to reduce speed → speeds unchanged. The result is the opposite of what the intervention aimed for. "However" signals this contradiction of expectation. A and D both mean "as a result" — wrong direction (the speed reduction didn't happen). B (In addition) = adding another point — wrong.
Example 3 — Contrast vs Concession distinctionHard
Proponents of ranked-choice voting argue that it reduces strategic voting and produces winners with broader support. _______, critics point out that the counting process is more complex and can delay the announcement of results.
💭 Thinking
S1: proponents say RCV is good. S2: critics say RCV has problems. Two groups with opposing views on the SAME thing. Is this contrast or concession? Contrast: two different things compared. Concession: acknowledging one side before countering. Here — two different groups are presented, each with their view. This is a contrast of perspectives. "While" or "however" both work here. Key: S2 doesn't start from accepting S1 — it's a separate viewpoint.
Which transition best completes the text?
  • A. Similarly
  • B. As a result
  • C. However
  • D. Specifically
Answer: C. Proponents say [good]. Critics say [bad]. Opposing views on the same policy = contrast. "However" signals the pivot to the opposing view. D (Specifically) = an example or elaboration — wrong. A (Similarly) = same direction — wrong. B (As a result) = causal — critics' concerns don't result from proponents' arguments.

Practice — 5 questions
Step 5
Practice 1
Early studies suggested that drinking coffee was associated with higher rates of heart disease. _______, a comprehensive meta-analysis of 36 studies found that moderate coffee consumption is actually associated with a lower risk of cardiovascular events.
Which transition best completes the text?
  • A. Furthermore
  • B. As a result
  • C. However
  • D. Similarly
C. Early studies said bad for heart → later research says good for heart. Direct reversal = contrast. "However" signals this.
Practice 2
The company's revenues grew by 22% in the third quarter. _______, its operating costs increased at an even faster rate, resulting in a net loss for the period.
Which transition best completes the text?
  • A. Consequently
  • B. Yet
  • C. For instance
  • D. Therefore
B. Revenue grew (positive signal) → costs grew faster, net loss (negative result despite positive signal). "Yet" contrasts the seemingly good revenue news with the bad profit news.
Practice 3
Traditional film cameras require careful exposure calculation for each shot, and mistakes cannot be corrected after the fact. _______, digital cameras allow photographers to review and adjust images immediately after capture.
Which transition best completes the text?
  • A. Additionally
  • B. Subsequently
  • C. By contrast
  • D. As a result
C. Film cameras [rigid, no correction] vs digital cameras [flexible, immediate review]. Two different things compared = contrast. "By contrast" names this directly.
Practice 4 — Hard
Introverts are often assumed to be poor leaders because leadership is associated with extroversion, assertiveness, and ease in social settings. _______, research by organizational psychologist Adam Grant has found that introverted leaders frequently outperform extroverted ones when managing proactive employees.
Which transition best completes the text?
  • A. Similarly
  • B. In fact
  • C. However
  • D. For this reason
C. Common assumption [introverts bad leaders] → research finding [introverts outperform in some cases]. The research contradicts the assumption. "However" signals this reversal. B (In fact) is tempting — it can introduce a surprising fact — but it often signals clarification/intensification, not a clean contrast between an assumption and evidence. "However" is more precise here.
Practice 5 — Hard (contrast vs concession)
Ancient Rome's engineering achievements — its roads, aqueducts, and concrete — were remarkable for their time and many have lasted two millennia. _______, Roman society was built on the systematic exploitation of enslaved people, whose forced labour made these achievements possible.
Which transition best completes the text?
  • A. Furthermore
  • B. As a result
  • C. However
  • D. Similarly
C. S1: Rome's engineering was remarkable. S2: Roman society was built on slavery. The second sentence complicates the admiration in the first — it's a "yes, but" move. This is actually closer to concession than pure contrast, but "However" correctly signals the pivot from the positive to the qualifying negative. A (Furthermore) would add another achievement — wrong. B would make slavery a result of engineering — wrong.

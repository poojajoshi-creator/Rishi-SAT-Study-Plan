Concept 16 / 27SEC — Punctuation
Commas
Commas have exactly four jobs on the SAT. Master the four, ignore everything else — including how the sentence sounds when you read it aloud.
~2–3 per moduleTarget: 35 secRule: identify the comma's job, then apply
,
The four jobs of a comma
Step 1
Job 1 — After an introductory element

When a sentence begins with a phrase or clause before the main subject, a comma separates the introduction from the main clause.
"After years of research, the scientists published their findings."
"Having studied all night, she felt prepared for the exam."
"Although the results were surprising, the team remained confident."

Job 2 — Around a non-restrictive (parenthetical) element

Extra information that could be removed without changing the sentence's core meaning gets commas on both sides.
"The treaty, which was signed in 1848, ended the war." (removing it still makes sense)
"Marie Curie, the first woman to win a Nobel Prize, was born in Poland."
Missing one comma of the pair is an error — both must be present.

Job 3 — Between items in a series

Three or more items need commas between them. The Oxford comma (before "and") is the SAT standard.
"The report covered food security, water access, and climate adaptation."
Only two items do NOT get a comma: "food security and water access" (no comma).

Job 4 — Before a coordinating conjunction joining independent clauses

Comma + FANBOYS (For/And/Nor/But/Or/Yet/So) joins two complete sentences.
"She submitted the application, and she received a response within a week."
If what follows "and" is NOT a complete sentence, no comma: "She submitted the application and received a response." (no comma — "received" shares the subject "She")

⭐
Strategy
Step 2
1
Identify which job the comma is supposed to do. Is it after an intro phrase? Around a parenthetical? Between list items? Before a FANBOYS?
2
Check if the job requires one comma or two. Parenthetical elements always need commas on BOTH sides. Introductory phrases need ONE comma after. Never use two commas when one is needed.
3
Never add a comma just because the sentence is long or because you'd pause when reading it aloud. Spoken rhythm is not a grammar rule.
⚠️
Traps
Step 3
Trap 1
Comma between subject and verb
Never put a comma between a subject and its verb: "The results of the experiment, were surprising." Wrong — the comma interrupts the core sentence. Remove it.
Trap 2
Missing the second comma
"The report, which was published in 2022 showed that..." — the closing comma after "2022" is missing. Parenthetical elements need both commas.
Trap 3
Comma before "that"
"That" introduces a restrictive clause — no commas. "The study that was published last year showed..." (no comma before "that"). "Which" introduces non-restrictive — commas needed.
Trap 4
Comma before "and" with shared subject
"She wrote the report and submitted it." — no comma because "submitted" shares the subject "She." Only use comma + and when what follows is a COMPLETE new sentence.

Worked examples
Step 4
Example 1 — Parenthetical missing second commaMedium
The Amazon River, which flows through nine South American countries carries more water than any other river on Earth.
Which choice correctly punctuates the sentence?
  • A. No change
  • B. countries, carries
  • C. countries carries
  • D. countries: carries
B. "Which flows through nine South American countries" is a non-restrictive clause — it needs commas on BOTH sides. The opening comma is there ("River,") but the closing comma is missing. B adds the closing comma after "countries." A (no change) leaves one comma — wrong. D (colon) — colons don't close parenthetical clauses.
Example 2 — Comma between subject and verbMedium
The discovery of penicillin by Alexander Fleming in 1928, revolutionised the treatment of bacterial infections worldwide.
Which choice correctly punctuates the sentence?
  • A. Remove the comma after "1928"
  • B. No change
  • C. Add a comma after "Fleming"
  • D. Add a comma after "penicillin"
A. The subject is "The discovery of penicillin by Alexander Fleming in 1928." The comma after "1928" incorrectly separates this subject from its verb "revolutionised." Remove the comma — no interruption between subject and verb.
Example 3 — Shared subject, no comma neededHard
The committee reviewed the proposal, and rejected it unanimously after a brief discussion.
Which choice correctly punctuates the sentence?
  • A. Remove the comma after "proposal"
  • B. No change
  • C. Replace the comma with a semicolon
  • D. Replace "and" with "however"
A. "Rejected it unanimously" shares the subject "The committee" — it's not a new independent clause. Comma + "and" before a dependent verb phrase (no new subject) is wrong. Remove the comma: "The committee reviewed the proposal and rejected it unanimously." No comma needed when the second verb shares the first subject.

Practice — 5 questions
Step 5
Practice 1
The three main causes of the financial crisis were excessive risk-taking poor regulatory oversight, and a lack of transparency in financial reporting.
Which choice correctly punctuates the series?
  • A. risk-taking, poor regulatory oversight, and
  • B. risk-taking poor regulatory oversight and
  • C. risk-taking, poor regulatory oversight and
  • D. risk-taking; poor regulatory oversight; and
A. Three items in a series: commas after items 1 and 2, including the Oxford comma before "and." B has no commas. C is missing the Oxford comma before "and." D uses semicolons — those are for complex lists where the items themselves contain commas.
Practice 2
Dr. Elena Vasquez who pioneered the technique has received numerous international awards for her contribution to regenerative medicine.
Which choice correctly punctuates the sentence?
  • A. No change
  • B. Vasquez, who pioneered the technique has
  • C. Vasquez, who pioneered the technique, has
  • D. Vasquez who pioneered the technique, has
C. "Who pioneered the technique" is a non-restrictive clause — it adds extra info about Vasquez but the sentence makes sense without it. Both commas required: one after "Vasquez" and one after "technique." B and D each have only one comma — wrong.
Practice 3
Having completed her doctoral dissertation after seven years of research, the professor submitted it to the examination board and waited for their response.
Which choice is correct?
  • A. No change — the sentence is correctly punctuated
  • B. research, the professor submitted it to the examination board, and waited
  • C. research the professor submitted it to the examination board and waited
  • D. research; the professor submitted it to the examination board and waited
A. The introductory participial phrase "Having completed her doctoral dissertation after seven years of research" is correctly followed by a comma. "Submitted...and waited" shares the subject "the professor" — no comma before "and" when the second verb has no new subject. The sentence is correct as written.
Practice 4
The study that was published in Nature last month, demonstrates that regular aerobic exercise can significantly reduce the risk of cognitive decline in adults over sixty.
Which choice correctly punctuates the sentence?
  • A. No change
  • B. Remove the comma after "month"
  • C. Add a comma after "that"
  • D. Replace the comma with a semicolon
B. "That was published in Nature last month" is a RESTRICTIVE clause (uses "that" not "which") — it identifies WHICH study. No commas around restrictive clauses. The comma after "month" incorrectly separates the subject from its verb "demonstrates." Remove it.
Practice 5 — Hard
In the early twentieth century urban planners, believing that density was inherently unhealthy, designed cities around the assumption that residents should be dispersed into low-density suburban developments.
Which choice is correct?
  • A. No change — the sentence is correctly punctuated
  • B. century urban planners believing that density was inherently unhealthy, designed
  • C. century, urban planners believing that density was inherently unhealthy designed
  • D. century, urban planners, believing that density was inherently unhealthy designed
A. The sentence correctly uses: (1) comma after the introductory phrase "In the early twentieth century"; (2) commas around the parenthetical "believing that density was inherently unhealthy." Three commas total — all correct. B removes the intro comma. C has the intro comma but only the opening parenthetical comma. D has both parenthetical commas but misses the intro comma AND the closing parenthetical one is in the wrong place.

Commas — Everything you need
4 jobs
After introductory element · Around non-restrictive clause (both commas) · Between series items · Before FANBOYS joining two independent clauses
Never
Between subject and verb · Before "that" (restrictive) · Before "and" when second verb shares the subject
That vs Which
"That" = restrictive = no commas. "Which" = non-restrictive = commas on both sides. Swap "that" for "which" only with commas.
Paired commas rule
Parenthetical elements ALWAYS need commas on BOTH sides. One comma = error. If you open the parenthesis, you must close it.

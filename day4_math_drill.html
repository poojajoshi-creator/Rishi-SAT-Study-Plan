Angles & Parallel Lines
Q1Angles — Parallel LinesMedium
Two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. One pair of alternate interior angles measures (4x + 10)° and (6x − 20)°. What is the value of x?
  • A 10
  • B 12
  • C 15
  • D 20
Correct: C — x = 15
Alternate interior angles are EQUAL when lines are parallel. 4x+10 = 6x−20 → 30 = 2x → x = 15. Check: 4(15)+10=70°, 6(15)−20=70° ✓. Trap: co-interior angles are supplementary (=180°); alternate interior angles are equal.
Q2Angles — Interior AnglesMedium
The interior angles of a pentagon sum to 540°. Four of the angles measure 95°, 110°, 120°, and 105°. What is the measure of the fifth angle?
  • A 100°
  • B 105°
  • C 110°
  • D 115°
Correct: C — 110°
Sum of all five = 540°. Known four: 95+110+120+105 = 430°. Fifth angle = 540−430 = 110°. Formula for sum of interior angles: (n−2)×180°. Pentagon: (5−2)×180 = 540°.
Q3Angles — Exterior AnglesHard
In triangle PQR, angle P = 42° and angle Q = 67°. Line segment QR is extended beyond R to point S. What is the measure of angle PRS (the exterior angle at R)?
  • A 71°
  • B 99°
  • C 104°
  • D 109°
Correct: D — 109°
Exterior angle theorem: the exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two non-adjacent interior angles. Exterior angle at R = angle P + angle Q = 42 + 67 = 109°. (Or: angle R = 180−42−67=71°; exterior = 180−71=109°.)
Q4Angles — Vertical & SupplementaryMedium
Two lines intersect forming four angles. One angle measures (3x + 25)°. The angle vertically opposite to it measures (5x − 15)°. What is the measure of each of the other two angles?
  • A 80°
  • B 90°
  • C 95°
  • D 100°
Correct: C — 95°
Vertical angles are equal: 3x+25 = 5x−15 → 40 = 2x → x = 20. One pair = 3(20)+25 = 85°. The other two angles are supplementary to 85°: 180−85 = 95°.
Triangles & Similarity
Q5Triangles — Pythagorean TheoremMedium
A ladder 17 metres long leans against a vertical wall. The base of the ladder is 8 metres from the wall. How high up the wall does the ladder reach?
  • A 13 m
  • B 14 m
  • C 15 m
  • D 16 m
Correct: C — 15 m
Pythagorean theorem: a²+b²=c². 8²+h²=17² → 64+h²=289 → h²=225 → h=15. Recognise: this is an 8-15-17 Pythagorean triple.
Q6Triangles — Similar TrianglesMedium
Triangles ABC and DEF are similar. In triangle ABC: AB = 8, BC = 12, AC = 10. In triangle DEF, DE = 20. What is the length of EF?
  • A 24
  • B 30
  • C 32
  • D 36
Correct: B — EF = 30
AB corresponds to DE. Scale factor = DE/AB = 20/8 = 5/2. EF corresponds to BC. EF = BC × (5/2) = 12 × 2.5 = 30.
Q7Triangles — 30-60-90Hard
In a 30-60-90 triangle, the hypotenuse has length 24. What is the length of the side opposite the 30° angle?
30-60-90 ratios: short leg : long leg : hypotenuse = 1 : √3 : 2
  • A 8
  • B 10
  • C 12
  • D 12√3
Correct: C — 12
Ratio: short : hypotenuse = 1:2. Short leg (opposite 30°) = hypotenuse/2 = 24/2 = 12. Long leg (opposite 60°) = 12√3.
Q8Triangles — Area & SimilarityHard
Two similar triangles have corresponding sides in the ratio 3:5. The area of the smaller triangle is 27 cm². What is the area of the larger triangle?
  • A 45 cm²
  • B 54 cm²
  • C 63 cm²
  • D 75 cm²
Correct: D — 75 cm²
Key rule: areas of similar figures scale by the SQUARE of the linear ratio. Linear ratio = 3:5. Area ratio = 3²:5² = 9:25. 27/area = 9/25 → area = 27×25/9 = 75 cm².
Circles
Q9Circles — Arc LengthMedium
A circle has circumference 48π cm. A sector of this circle has a central angle of 75°. What is the arc length of this sector?
  • A 8π cm
  • B 9π cm
  • C 10π cm
  • D 12π cm
Correct: C — 10π cm
Arc length = (θ/360°) × circumference = (75/360) × 48π = (5/24) × 48π = 10π cm.
Q10Circles — Sector AreaMedium
A circle has radius 12 cm. What is the area of a sector with central angle 120°? Give your answer in terms of π.
  • A 36π cm²
  • B 42π cm²
  • C 48π cm²
  • D 54π cm²
Correct: C — 48π cm²
Sector area = (θ/360°) × πr² = (120/360) × π(12²) = (1/3) × 144π = 48π cm².
Q11Circles — Equation of a CircleHard
Which of the following is the centre and radius of the circle defined by x² + y² − 6x + 8y − 11 = 0?
  • A Centre (3, −4), radius = 6
  • B Centre (3, −4), radius = √36 = 6
  • C Centre (−3, 4), radius = 6
  • D Centre (3, −4), radius = √24
Correct: B — Centre (3, −4), r = 6
Complete the square: x²−6x + y²+8y = 11. x: (x−3)²−9; y: (y+4)²−16. So (x−3)²+(y+4)²= 11+9+16 = 36. Centre = (3,−4), r = √36 = 6.
Q12Circles — Inscribed AnglesHard
An inscribed angle in a circle intercepts an arc of 160°. What is the measure of the inscribed angle?
  • A 40°
  • B 60°
  • C 80°
  • D 160°
Correct: C — 80°
Inscribed angle theorem: inscribed angle = ½ × intercepted arc. 160°/2 = 80°. The central angle would equal the arc (160°); the inscribed angle is always half. Trap: don't confuse inscribed angle with central angle.
Volume & Surface Area
Q13Volume — CylinderMedium
A cylinder has a diameter of 10 cm and a height of 14 cm. What is its volume in terms of π?
  • A 140π cm³
  • B 280π cm³
  • C 350π cm³
  • D 700π cm³
Correct: C — 350π cm³
V = πr²h. Diameter = 10 → r = 5. V = π(5²)(14) = π(25)(14) = 350π cm³. Trap: use radius (5), not diameter (10).
Q14Volume — Cone vs CylinderMedium
A cone and a cylinder share the same base radius of 6 cm. The cylinder has height 9 cm. If the cone has the same volume as the cylinder, what is the height of the cone?
  • A 9 cm
  • B 18 cm
  • C 27 cm
  • D 36 cm
Correct: C — 27 cm
V(cylinder) = πr²h = π(36)(9) = 324π. V(cone) = ⅓πr²h = ⅓π(36)(h) = 12πh. Set equal: 12πh = 324π → h = 27. Since a cone holds ⅓ the volume of a same-dimension cylinder, it needs 3× the height to hold the same volume.
Q15Volume — SphereHard
A sphere has a diameter of 6 cm. What is its volume? (V = 4/3 πr³)
  • A 36π cm³
  • B 36π cm³ → actually 36π
  • C 72π cm³
  • D 288π cm³
Correct: B — 36π cm³
Diameter = 6 → r = 3. V = (4/3)πr³ = (4/3)π(27) = 36π cm³. Trap: use r = 3, not d = 6. (4/3)(27) = 36.
Q16Area — Composite ShapeHard
A rectangle measures 12 cm by 8 cm. A semicircle with diameter equal to the width of the rectangle (8 cm) is attached to one of the shorter ends. What is the total area of the composite shape, in terms of π?
  • A 96 + 8π cm²
  • B 96 + 16π cm²
  • C 96 + 8π cm²
  • D 96 + 32π cm²
Correct: C — 96 + 8π cm²
Rectangle area = 12×8 = 96 cm². Semicircle: diameter = 8, so r = 4. Area of semicircle = ½πr² = ½π(16) = 8π. Total = 96 + 8π cm².
Trigonometry
Q17Trig — SOHCAHTOAMedium
In right triangle ABC, angle C = 90°, angle A = 38°, and the hypotenuse AB = 20 cm. What is the length of side BC (opposite to angle A), to the nearest tenth? (sin 38° ≈ 0.616)
  • A 10.8 cm
  • B 11.5 cm
  • C 12.3 cm
  • D 15.7 cm
Correct: C — 12.3 cm
sin A = opposite/hypotenuse = BC/AB. BC = AB × sin(38°) = 20 × 0.616 = 12.32 ≈ 12.3 cm.
Q18Trig — Special ValuesMedium
What is the value of sin(30°) × cos(60°) + cos(30°) × sin(60°)?
  • A 0
  • B 1/2
  • C √3/2
  • D 1
Correct: D — 1
Recognise the sine addition formula: sin(A+B) = sinA cosB + cosA sinB. This equals sin(30°+60°) = sin(90°) = 1. Or compute directly: (1/2)(1/2) + (√3/2)(√3/2) = 1/4 + 3/4 = 1.
Q19Trig — Complementary IdentityHard
If cos(θ) = 5/13, and θ is an acute angle, what is tan(θ)?
  • A 5/12
  • B 12/13
  • C 12/5
  • D 13/12
Correct: C — tan θ = 12/5
cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse = 5/13. This is a 5-12-13 Pythagorean triple. Opposite = 12. sin θ = 12/13. tan θ = opposite/adjacent = 12/5.
Q20Trig — Radian MeasuresHard
An angle measures 5π/6 radians. What is its measure in degrees, and what is its sine value?
  • A 120°, sin = √3/2
  • B 150°, sin = −1/2
  • C 150°, sin = 1/2
  • D 135°, sin = √2/2
Correct: C — 150°, sin = 1/2
Convert: (5π/6) × (180/π) = 5×30 = 150°. sin(150°) = sin(180°−30°) = sin(30°) = 1/2. (150° is in the second quadrant where sine is positive.) Trap: sin(150°) ≠ −1/2 — that's cos(120°).

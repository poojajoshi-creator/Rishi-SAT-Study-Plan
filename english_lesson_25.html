Concept 25 / 27SEC — Grammar
Parallelism
Items in a list or comparison must use the same grammatical form. If the first item is a verb, all items must be verbs. If the first is a noun, all must be nouns. The form must match — every single time.
~1–2 per moduleTarget: 40 secRule: same role = same form throughout
∥
What is parallelism?
Step 1
The core rule

When words, phrases, or clauses are joined by coordinating conjunctions (and, but, or) or appear in a list, they must all use the same grammatical form.

✗ "She enjoys hiking, to swim, and read books." — Three different forms
✓ "She enjoys hiking, swimming, and reading books." — All gerunds
✓ "She enjoys to hike, to swim, and to read books." — All infinitives

✗ "The report was thorough, well-organised, and it covered all the key points." — Two adjectives then a clause
✓ "The report was thorough, well-organised, and comprehensive." — Three adjectives

Three structures that demand parallelism

1. Lists: "The plan required funding, staffing, and to train new personnel." → "funding, staffing, and training new personnel."

2. Comparisons (than / as ... as): "Running a marathon is harder than to cycle 100 miles." → "Running a marathon is harder than cycling 100 miles." Both sides of "than" must match.

3. Correlative conjunctions: both...and · either...or · neither...nor · not only...but also · whether...or
"She is not only a gifted musician but also she writes poetry." → "not only a gifted musician but also a poet." Both sides of the correlative must be the same form.

⭐
Strategy
Step 2
1
Find the list or comparison. Look for "and," "or," "but," "than," or correlative pairs (both...and, not only...but also).
2
Identify the form of the first item. Is it a noun? A gerund (-ing)? An infinitive (to + verb)? A past participle? A clause (subject + verb)?
3
Make every other item match that form. If the first is a gerund, all must be gerunds. If the first is a noun phrase, all must be noun phrases. Adjust only the underlined portion — not the items you're not being asked about.
⚠️
Traps
Step 3
Trap 1
Gerund vs infinitive mix
"The company focuses on reducing costs, improving efficiency, and to expand into new markets." → "expanding." The first two are gerunds — all must be gerunds.
Trap 2
Clause slipped into a noun list
"The benefits include lower taxes, simplified regulation, and businesses will have access to new markets." → "access to new markets." A full clause can't sit alongside noun phrases in a list.
Trap 3
Correlative pair misalignment
"She is not only known for her research but also her teaching is excellent." → "but also for her excellent teaching." What follows "not only" and "but also" must be grammatically parallel.
Trap 4
Comparison both sides
"Analysing data takes more skill than to guess." → "guessing." Both sides of "than" must match: "Analysing" (gerund) vs "guessing" (gerund).

Worked examples
Step 4
Example 1 — Gerund listEasy
The training programme focuses on building technical skills, fostering teamwork, and to develop leadership capacity among junior staff.
  • A. No change
  • B. developing
  • C. the development of
  • D. develop
B. "Building" and "fostering" are both gerunds. The third item must also be a gerund: "developing." A (to develop) = infinitive — wrong form. C (the development of) = noun phrase — different structure. D (develop) = bare verb — wrong form.
Example 2 — Not only...but alsoMedium
The vaccine not only reduces the severity of symptoms but also patients are protected against long-term complications.
  • A. No change
  • B. there is protection against long-term complications for patients
  • C. protects against long-term complications
  • D. offering protection against long-term complications
C. "Not only [reduces]...but also [?]." "Reduces" is a verb — "but also" must introduce a matching verb. C: "protects" — parallel verb. A = full clause after "but also" — breaks the parallel structure. B = passive clause — wrong form. D = participle phrase — changes the grammatical structure.
Example 3 — Comparison with "than"Hard
Investing in preventive healthcare is far more cost-effective than to treat chronic conditions after they develop.
  • A. No change
  • B. treating
  • C. the treatment of
  • D. when you treat
B. "Investing" (gerund) is more cost-effective than "?" — both sides of "than" must match. "Treating" (gerund) is parallel. A (to treat) = infinitive — wrong. C (the treatment of) = noun phrase — different grammatical structure. D (when you treat) = adverbial clause — wrong form and introduces an unnecessary pronoun.

Practice — 5 questions
Step 5
Practice 1
The city's new sustainability plan aims to reduce carbon emissions, increase green spaces, and the improvement of public transport links.
  • A. No change
  • B. improve public transport links
  • C. improving public transport links
  • D. to improve public transport links
B. "Reduce" and "increase" are bare infinitive forms (after "to" at the start: "to reduce...increase...and improve"). All three must be bare verbs. B: "improve" — matches. C (improving) = gerund. D (to improve) = double infinitive ("to" is already shared). A = noun phrase — wrong form.
Practice 2
The ideal candidate will have experience in data analysis, project management, and how to communicate effectively with stakeholders.
  • A. No change
  • B. communicating effectively with stakeholders
  • C. stakeholder communication
  • D. to communicate effectively with stakeholders
C. "Data analysis" and "project management" are noun phrases. The third item must also be a noun phrase. C: "stakeholder communication" — matches perfectly. A = embedded question clause — wrong. B = gerund phrase — not a simple noun phrase. D = infinitive — wrong.
Practice 3
The critics praised the film for its stunning visuals, its emotionally resonant score, and because the screenplay was intelligent.
  • A. No change
  • B. the screenplay being intelligent
  • C. its intelligent screenplay
  • D. an intelligent screenplay
C. "Its stunning visuals" and "its emotionally resonant score" are possessive noun phrases with "its." The third must match: "its intelligent screenplay." A = subordinate clause — completely wrong form. B = participial noun phrase — different structure. D = article + noun — loses the "its" that matches the parallel structure.
Practice 4 — Not only...but also
The new drug not only slows the progression of the disease but also there are fewer side effects than conventional treatments.
  • A. No change
  • B. produces fewer side effects than conventional treatments do
  • C. produces fewer side effects than conventional treatments
  • D. causing fewer side effects than conventional treatments
C. "Not only [slows]...but also [?]." Slows = simple present verb. Must match: "produces" (C). B also has a matching verb "produces" — but adds "do" at the end which is unnecessary and makes the sentence awkward. C is cleaner and correct. A = existential clause — wrong. D = participle — wrong form for correlative conjunction parallelism.
Practice 5 — Hard
The architect's design was praised for being innovative, sustainable, and it integrated seamlessly with the surrounding landscape.
  • A. No change
  • B. because it integrated seamlessly with the surrounding landscape
  • C. seamlessly integrated with the surrounding landscape
  • D. integrating seamlessly with the surrounding landscape
C. "Innovative" and "sustainable" are adjectives following "being." The third item must also be an adjective or adjective phrase: "seamlessly integrated with the surrounding landscape" — a past participial adjective phrase. A = full clause — wrong. B = subordinate clause — wrong. D = present participle — "integrating" doesn't function as an adjective here in the same way as "innovative" and "sustainable."

Summary card
Keep this
Parallelism — Everything you need
The rule
Items joined by and/or/but in a list or comparison must use the same grammatical form. Check: noun, gerund, infinitive, adjective, clause — all items must match.
Step 1
Find the first item. Name its form. Then check every other item matches that form exactly.
Correlative conjunctions
both...and · either...or · neither...nor · not only...but also · whether...or — what follows each half must be grammatically identical.
Comparisons (than / as...as)
Both sides of "than" or "as...as" must match. Gerund than gerund. Noun than noun. Never gerund than infinitive.
Common fix
If the list starts with gerunds (-ing), make the odd one out a gerund. If it starts with bare verbs, make all bare verbs. If noun phrases, make all noun phrases.
Trap
A full clause (subject + verb) slipped into a list of nouns or gerunds is always wrong. "The plan involves funding, staffing, and employees will be trained" → "training employees."

Why Data Analysis is winnable

Data Analysis dropped to 550–600 in June. Unlike Advanced Math (which requires algebraic fluency), Data Analysis is mostly about reading carefully and applying straightforward formulas. These are not hard concepts — they're concepts that require careful, methodical reading rather than mathematical insight.

The most common reason students lose points here: they rush. They misread the table, misidentify what percentage they're calculating, or confuse "probability of X given Y" with "probability of X." Slow down on these questions. 45 seconds of careful reading saves a wrong answer.

1

Percentages & Percentage Change

Formulas
Percentage of a total
Part ÷ Whole × 100
Percentage change
(New − Old) ÷ Old × 100  → positive = increase, negative = decrease
Finding the original value
If X is P% of Y, then Y = X ÷ (P/100)

The SAT tests three percentage scenarios: finding what percent one number is of another, finding a percentage change between two values, and working backwards from a percentage to find the original.

Example — Percentage Change
A store's revenue increased from $240,000 to $312,000. Percentage change = (312,000 − 240,000) ÷ 240,000 × 100 = 72,000 ÷ 240,000 × 100 = 30% increase
Common trap — which is the base?
In percentage change, always divide by the original (old) value, not the new one. "A price increased from $80 to $100" → (100−80)÷80 = 25%, NOT (100−80)÷100 = 20%.

Two-Way Tables

How to read them
Two-way tables show the distribution of two categorical variables. Questions ask for counts, proportions, or conditional probabilities. The key: always identify what the question is asking you to use as the denominator.
Supports policyOpposes policyTotal
Urban340160500
Rural120280400
Total460440900
Q: What fraction of all respondents support the policy?
460 ÷ 900 = 51.1%  → denominator = total (900)
Q: Of urban respondents, what fraction support the policy?
340 ÷ 500 = 68%  → denominator = urban total (500), not grand total
Q: Of those who support the policy, what fraction are urban?
340 ÷ 460 = 73.9%  → denominator = support total (460)
The denominator trap — most common error
The SAT deliberately asks questions where using the wrong total gives a plausible-looking wrong answer. Always re-read the question: "Of all respondents" vs "of urban respondents" vs "of those who support" — each is a different denominator.

Mean, Median, Mode & Standard Deviation

Formulas & Rules
Mean (average)
Sum of all values ÷ number of values
Median
Middle value when sorted. Even count: average of middle two.
Mode
Most frequently occurring value.
Standard deviation measures how spread out data values are from the mean. You don't need to calculate it on the SAT — just know: larger SD = more spread out, smaller SD = more clustered around the mean.
Skewed distributions
Right skew (tail right): Mean > Median
Left skew (tail left): Mean < Median
Symmetric: Mean ≈ Median
Effect of outliers
Outliers pull the mean toward them. The median is resistant to outliers. Adding a very large value = mean increases more than median.
The "adding a value" trap
If a new value is added to a data set that equals the current mean, the mean stays the same. If the new value is above the mean, the mean increases. If below, it decreases. Always reason through this rather than guessing.
4

Scatterplots & Line of Best Fit

What the SAT tests
Scatterplot questions ask you to read data points, interpret the line (or curve) of best fit, and understand what the slope and intercept mean in context.
Reading the equation y = mx + b
m = slope = how much y changes for each 1-unit increase in x (the rate of change)
b = y-intercept = the predicted value of y when x = 0
Interpreting context
If y = 2.5x + 40 models weekly sales (y) vs. advertising spend (x in $100s): slope means "each additional $100 in advertising predicts 2.5 more sales per week." Intercept = predicted sales with zero advertising.
Positive correlationAs x increases, y increases. Line slopes up.
Negative correlationAs x increases, y decreases. Line slopes down.
Strong correlationPoints cluster tightly around the line.
Weak correlationPoints are widely scattered around the line.
Correlation ≠ causation — always tested
A scatterplot showing a strong correlation does NOT prove that one variable causes the other. The SAT will ask "which conclusion is supported by the data?" — always eliminate any answer that says one variable causes the other based on correlation alone.
5

Probability

Formulas
Basic probability
P(event) = Number of favourable outcomes ÷ Total possible outcomes
Conditional probability — P(A given B)
P(A|B) = P(A and B) ÷ P(B)  → on SAT: favourable ÷ restricted total
Complementary probability
P(not A) = 1 − P(A)

Most SAT probability questions involve tables or frequency counts rather than complex probability theory. The key is identifying the correct sample space (total) for the probability you're calculating.

Conditional probability example
Using the table from the Two-Way Tables section: P(supports policy | urban) = 340 ÷ 500 = 0.68. The "given urban" restricts the sample space to 500 urban respondents, not 900 total.
6

Sampling & Study Design

Key concepts
Random sample
Every member of the population has an equal chance of being selected. Results can be generalised to the full population. If NOT random → cannot generalise.
Sample size
Larger random samples → more reliable estimates → smaller margin of error. Doubling sample size does NOT double precision — it reduces the margin of error by a factor of √2.
Generalising from a sample
You can only generalise results to the population the sample was drawn from. If a study sampled high school students in one city, findings cannot be generalised to "all teenagers in the country."
Experiments vs. observational studies
Only randomised controlled experiments (with random assignment to treatment/control) can establish causation. Observational studies can show correlation but cannot prove cause and effect.
The generalisation trap — very commonly tested
If a study is conducted on a non-random or specific sample (e.g., "volunteers at one gym"), the SAT will ask which conclusion is supported. Eliminate any answer that generalises to a broader population not represented by the sample.

Practice — 15 Questions

Q1 — Percentage Change
A company's annual profit increased from $1.2 million to $1.56 million over three years. By what percentage did the profit increase?
  • A23.1%
  • B26.9%
  • C30%
  • D36%
Correct: C — 30%. (1.56 − 1.2) ÷ 1.2 × 100 = 0.36 ÷ 1.2 × 100 = 0.3 × 100 = 30%. Note: divide by the OLD value (1.2), not the new one.

Of the students who failed the exam, what fraction did not attend tutoring?
  • A55/200
  • B55/100
  • C55/71
  • D16/71
Correct: C — 55/71. "Of the students who failed" restricts the denominator to the 71 who failed. Of those 71, 55 had no tutoring. → 55/71. A uses the grand total (wrong). B uses the no-tutoring row total (wrong). D = 16/71 = those who attended tutoring and failed (not what was asked).
Q3 — Mean
The mean score of 8 students on a test is 74. A ninth student joins the group and scores 92. What is the new mean score for all 9 students?
  • A76
  • B76
  • C78
  • D83
Correct: B — 76. Total for 8 students = 8 × 74 = 592. Add 9th student: 592 + 92 = 684. New mean = 684 ÷ 9 = 76. Always reconstruct the total sum from the mean × count, then add the new value and divide by the new count.
Q4 — Median & Outliers
A data set has 7 values with a mean of 50 and a median of 48. An eighth value of 150 is added to the set. Which of the following best describes the effect on the mean and median?
  • ABoth mean and median increase significantly.
  • BThe mean increases significantly; the median increases only slightly or stays similar.
  • CBoth mean and median are unaffected by the outlier.
  • DThe median increases significantly; the mean is unaffected.
Correct: B. Outliers (extreme values) strongly affect the mean but have little effect on the median. Adding 150 to a set where mean=50 pulls the mean up substantially. The median shifts only because a new middle position is established — it may rise slightly but not dramatically. The mean is not resistant to outliers; the median is.
Q5 — Standard Deviation
Two classes both have a mean score of 70. Class A has scores ranging from 65 to 75. Class B has scores ranging from 40 to 100. Which statement is correct?
  • AClass A has a higher standard deviation than Class B.
  • BBoth classes have the same standard deviation because they have the same mean.
  • CClass B has a higher standard deviation than Class A.
  • DStandard deviation cannot be compared between two different classes.
Correct: C. Standard deviation measures spread, not average. Class B's scores range from 40 to 100 — much more spread out than Class A's 65 to 75. Therefore Class B has a higher SD. B is wrong — same mean does NOT mean same SD. The mean and SD are independent measures.
Q6 — Scatterplot / Line of Best Fit
A scatterplot shows a line of best fit with the equation y = 3.2x + 18, where x is the number of hours of weekly exercise and y is a wellness score. What does the slope 3.2 represent?
  • AThe wellness score for someone who exercises 0 hours per week.
  • BThe total wellness score for all participants.
  • CThe predicted increase in wellness score for each additional hour of weekly exercise.
  • DThe number of hours of exercise that produces the maximum wellness score.
Correct: C. The slope (3.2) = the rate of change = how much y increases for each 1-unit increase in x. Here: each additional hour of weekly exercise predicts a 3.2-point increase in wellness score. A = describes the y-intercept (18), not the slope. B and D are nonsensical interpretations.
Q7 — Correlation vs. Causation
A study finds a strong positive correlation between the number of books in a child's home and the child's reading test score. Which of the following conclusions is most justified by this finding?
  • AHaving more books in the home causes children to score higher on reading tests.
  • BBuying more books will always improve a child's reading test score.
  • CChildren in homes with more books tend to score higher on reading tests.
  • DReading test scores determine how many books families purchase.
Correct: C. Correlation justifies statements about tendency ("tend to"), not causation ("causes"). C accurately reflects the correlation without claiming cause. A = uses "causes" — not supported by correlation alone. B = "always" is too strong. D = reverses the direction without evidence.
Q8 — Probability
In a bag containing 5 red, 8 blue, and 7 green marbles, what is the probability of selecting a marble that is NOT red?
  • A5/20
  • B8/20
  • C15/20
  • D13/20
Correct: C — 15/20 (= 3/4). Total marbles = 5 + 8 + 7 = 20. NOT red = 8 + 7 = 15. P(not red) = 15/20. Alternatively: P(not red) = 1 − P(red) = 1 − 5/20 = 15/20.

Q10 — Sampling / Generalisation
A researcher surveys 200 customers who visit a specific coffee shop on weekday mornings and finds that 85% prefer hot drinks over cold drinks. Which conclusion is best supported by this data?
  • A85% of all adults in the city prefer hot drinks.
  • BHot drinks will always outsell cold drinks at coffee shops.
  • CAmong weekday morning customers at this coffee shop, the majority prefer hot drinks.
  • DCold drinks should be removed from this coffee shop's menu.
Correct: C. The sample is weekday morning customers at one specific coffee shop — findings can only be generalised to that specific population. C accurately limits the conclusion to "weekday morning customers at this coffee shop." A = generalises to all city adults (not the population sampled). B = "always" is too strong and generalises to all coffee shops. D = a policy recommendation not supported by data alone.
Q11 — Percentage of Total
A school has 840 students. If 35% are in Grade 9, 25% in Grade 10, and 20% in Grade 11, how many students are in Grade 12?
  • A126
  • B147
  • C168
  • D210
Correct: C — 168. Grade 12 = 100% − 35% − 25% − 20% = 20% of 840. 0.20 × 840 = 168.
Q12 — Line of Best Fit / Prediction
A line of best fit for a set of data is given by y = −1.5x + 60, where x is the number of days since launch and y is the number of daily app downloads (in thousands). According to this model, approximately how many days after launch will daily downloads reach 15,000?
  • A20 days
  • B25 days
  • C30 days
  • D40 days
Correct: C — 30 days. Set y = 15 (15,000 downloads in thousands): 15 = −1.5x + 60 → 1.5x = 45 → x = 30 days.

Q14 — Sampling Design
A researcher wants to determine the average number of hours per week that teenagers in the United States spend on social media. Which sampling method would produce the most reliable estimate?
  • ASurvey the first 500 teenagers who respond to an online advertisement.
  • BSurvey all students at one high school in California.
  • CRandomly select 1,000 teenagers from across all 50 states using a national database.
  • DSurvey 5,000 teenagers who follow a social media awareness account.
Correct: C. For reliable generalisation to "all US teenagers," the sample must be random and representative. C uses random selection from a national database across all states — best represents the full population. A = voluntary response (biased toward those who notice ads). B = only one school in one state (not representative). D = large but biased (followers of a social media awareness account likely have different usage patterns).
Q15 — Hard · Mixed
A data set of 10 values has a mean of 30 and a median of 28. If the two largest values are removed and both are equal to 50, what happens to the mean and median of the remaining 8 values?
  • ABoth mean and median decrease.
  • BThe mean decreases more than the median.
  • CThe mean decreases; the median increases.
  • DBoth mean and median stay the same.
Correct: B. Mean: original total = 10 × 30 = 300. Remove two 50s: 300 − 100 = 200. New mean = 200 ÷ 8 = 25. The mean dropped from 30 to 25. Median: removing the two largest values shifts the median position to the 4th and 5th values of 8, which were already near the centre of the original set. The median was 28 (average of 5th and 6th of 10); now it's the average of 4th and 5th of 8 — these values were likely below 28, so median decreases somewhat, but less dramatically than the mean. B correctly identifies that the mean falls more than the median when large outliers are removed.

Data Analysis Quick-Reference

Seven types · key rules

Percentage change
(New − Old) ÷ Old × 100. Always divide by the OLD value.
Two-way tables
Identify the denominator first. "Of those who X" → row or column total, not grand total.
Mean vs Median
Outliers pull mean, not median. Reconstruct total sum = mean × count before adding/removing values.
Standard deviation
Larger SD = more spread. Same mean ≠ same SD. You won't calculate it — just compare spread.
Scatterplots
Slope = rate of change per 1-unit increase in x. y-intercept = value when x = 0. Correlation ≠ causation.
Probability
P = favourable ÷ total. Conditional: restrict denominator to the "given" group.
Sampling
Random sample → can generalise. Non-random → cannot generalise beyond the sample. Larger sample → smaller margin of error.
Correlation vs causation
Observational studies = correlation only. Only randomised experiments can show causation.

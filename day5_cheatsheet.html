English — Reading & Writing
Transition Logic — The 5 Types
Contrast However, Yet, In contrast, Despite, Nevertheless, Even so, Still, But, Although
Cause/Result Therefore, Thus, Consequently, As a result, Hence, So
Addition Furthermore, Moreover, Additionally, Also, In addition, Similarly
Concession Nevertheless, Even so, Yet, Still — used when S1 concedes and S2 defends
Example For instance, For example, Specifically, In particular
Concession signal words in S1: "It is true that / Admittedly / Although / It must be acknowledged / While" → S2 needs Nevertheless / Even so / Yet
Rhetorical Synthesis — 4 Steps
  • 1. Read the GOAL first — the question tells you exactly what the sentence must do
  • 2. Identify the MUST-HAVES — if goal says "both X and Y", the answer must have both
  • 3. Eliminate answers that are too broad or too narrow — match the goal precisely
  • 4. Correct answer often has "although/while/yet" — when goal asks for tension or nuance
Common traps: A (too general) · B (only one half of the goal) · D (policy/response instead of mechanism)
Add / Delete / Revise — Decision Tree
  • DELETE if: True but doesn't serve THIS paragraph's specific focus — etymology, origin stories, tangential details
  • KEEP if: Provides the causal link between S1 and S3 · Substantiates the opening claim · Provides the mechanism behind a general claim
  • PLACEMENT: "This X / These effects / This capacity" → find what X refers to and place IMMEDIATELY after
  • REVISE goal: "more explicitly" / "more analytical" = add the WHY/mechanism, not just restate the what
SEC — Must-Know Rules
  • FANBOYS — For And Nor But Or Yet So → join independent clauses with comma before: ", but"
  • Semicolons → join two independent clauses: "clause; clause" or "clause; however, clause"
  • Paired dashes — open with — must close with — (not comma, not semicolon)
  • Neither/nor · Either/or → verb agrees with the CLOSER subject
  • Dangling modifier → the doer of the opening phrase must be the subject: "Having studied X, the researcher..." not "Having studied X, the results..."
  • Parallelism → "to analyse, construct, and evaluate" (not "evaluating")
  • Conciseness → "the reason...was because" = redundant. "prior to" = before. "in nature" = delete.

Exam Day — What to Remember
TransitionsRead both sentences, identify the direction. Label S1 and S2 before looking at options.
SynthesisRead the GOAL first. Answers that only do half the job are wrong, even if they sound good.
Add/DeleteAsk: does this sentence serve THIS paragraph's specific focus? Not "is it true?" — is it relevant HERE?
Cross-TextT2 either confirms, contradicts, complicates, or extends T1. Identify which before answering.
Math trapsArc length uses circumference (2πr), not area. Cone = ⅓ cylinder. Use r not d. sin≠cos.
If stuckEliminate the obviously wrong, make your best call, move on. Don't let one question cost you three.

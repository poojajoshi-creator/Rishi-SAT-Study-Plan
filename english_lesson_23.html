Verb Tense
& Consistency
Tense tells the reader when events happen. The SAT tests two things: using the right tense for the timeline described, and keeping tense consistent when no shift is needed.
~1–2 per moduleTarget: 40 secAsk: when does this event happen relative to others?
t→
The tense system
Step 1
The key tenses and when to use them

Simple past (walked): event completed in the past. "She walked to the lab."

Past perfect (had walked): event completed BEFORE another past event. "She had already walked to the lab by the time the results arrived." Use "had + past participle" when one past event precedes another.

Simple present (walks): ongoing facts, habitual actions, or universal truths. "Water freezes at 0°C." (always true — present tense even in historical writing)

Present perfect (has walked): action that started in the past and continues or is relevant to the present. "Scientists have studied this phenomenon for decades."

Sequence words that signal tense:
"By the time..." / "Before..." / "After...had..." → past perfect
"Since..." / "For the past X years..." → present perfect
"Now that..." → present or future

⭐
Strategy
Step 2
1
Identify the established tense of the passage. Is it past? Present? Future? The surrounding verbs tell you.
2
Look for sequence words that signal a timeline: "before," "after," "by the time," "since," "until." These words determine the correct tense.
3
Consistency rule: unless the timeline demands a shift, keep the same tense throughout. An unexplained tense shift mid-passage is always an error.
4
Past perfect = had + past participle. Use it when one past event happened BEFORE another past event. "She studied before she took the exam" (simple past — both events). "She had studied extensively before she took the exam" (past perfect — study came first).
⚠️
Traps
Step 3
Trap 1
Missing past perfect
"By the time the rescue team arrived, the survivors crossed the river." "By the time" signals that "crossing" happened first — needs past perfect: "had crossed."
Trap 2
Unnecessary tense shift
Passage is in past tense. Mid-sentence: "she picks up the report and examines it carefully." Shift to present for no reason. Should stay: "she picked up the report and examined it."
Trap 3
Present for a completed past event
"Darwin observes finches in the Galápagos and develops his theory." If describing a historical event in narrative, use past: "Darwin observed... and developed."
Trap 4
Simple past instead of present perfect
"Since 1990, global temperatures rose by 1.2°C." "Since" signals a continuing period → present perfect: "have risen." Simple past implies the rising is complete; present perfect signals it continues.

Worked examples
Step 4
Example 1 — Past perfect requiredMedium
By the time the investigation was formally launched, key witnesses left the country and crucial documents were no longer accessible.
  • A. No change
  • B. had left
  • C. have left
  • D. were leaving
B. "By the time the investigation was launched" = the witnesses leaving happened BEFORE the investigation. One past event (leaving) before another past event (launching) = past perfect: "had left." C (have left) = present perfect — wrong tense for an entirely past timeline. D (were leaving) = past progressive — implies simultaneous ongoing action, not prior completion.
Example 2 — Tense consistencyMedium
The expedition set out in early March, crossed three mountain ranges, and arrives at the research station six weeks later.
  • A. No change
  • B. arrived
  • C. had arrived
  • D. was arriving
B. "Set out" and "crossed" are both simple past. The series must be consistent — "arrived" (simple past). A ("arrives") is an unexplained shift to present. C (had arrived) = past perfect — implies arriving happened before the crossing, which reverses the intended timeline.
Example 3 — Since + present perfectHard
Since the discovery of CRISPR technology in 2012, researchers made significant advances in gene editing for treating genetic diseases.
  • A. No change
  • B. have made
  • C. had made
  • D. make
B. "Since 2012" = from a past point up to the present → present perfect: "have made." A (made) = simple past — implies the advances are over. C (had made) = past perfect — implies the advances happened before something else in the past. D (make) = simple present — wrong tense. "Since" always signals present perfect when describing a continuing period.

Practice — 5 questions
Step 5
Practice 1
The scientist reported her findings, shared them with colleagues, and requests additional funding for a follow-up study.
  • A. No change
  • B. requested
  • C. had requested
  • D. was requesting
B. "Reported" and "shared" are simple past. Consistency: "requested." A (requests) = unjustified present shift. C (had requested) = past perfect — implies the request happened before reporting and sharing, which contradicts the sequence.
Practice 2
Before the bridge collapsed, engineers had warned city officials about its deteriorating structural integrity for over a decade.
  • A. No change — the sentence is correct
  • B. had collapsed
  • C. was collapsing
  • D. collapses
A. Correct as written. The warnings ("had warned") happened BEFORE the collapse. So warnings = past perfect. The collapse = simple past ("collapsed"). The sequence is properly expressed: past perfect (warnings first) → simple past (collapse second).
Practice 3
For the past two decades, the glacier retreated by an average of forty metres per year.
  • A. No change
  • B. has retreated
  • C. had retreated
  • D. retreats
B. "For the past two decades" = from the past to the present, ongoing → present perfect: "has retreated." A (retreated) = simple past — implies the retreat is complete. C (had retreated) = past perfect — would imply it stopped before something else. D (retreats) = simple present — wrong for a specific ongoing trend.
Practice 4
After the final vote was cast, the chairperson announced that the motion had passed by a narrow margin.
  • A. No change — the sentence is correct
  • B. had been cast
  • C. is cast
  • D. has been cast
A. "Was cast" (simple past) works here because "After" already establishes the sequence — the vote happened before the announcement. You don't need past perfect when "after" already clarifies the sequence. "Had been cast" (B) is grammatically possible but unnecessary. A is correct as written.
Practice 5 — Hard
The author writes the manuscript during a period of intense personal turmoil, drawing on memories she had suppressed for years, and published it to widespread critical acclaim.
  • A. No change
  • B. wrote
  • C. has written
  • D. had written
B. "Published" = simple past. Consistency: "wrote." The historical sequence is: suppressed memories (past perfect — before writing) → wrote the manuscript (simple past) → published (simple past). A (writes) = unjustified present tense shift. D (had written) = past perfect — implies writing happened before publishing but before something else too, creating an illogical three-layer sequence.

Summary card
Keep this
Verb Tense & Consistency — Everything you need
Past perfect (had + verb)
Use when one past event happened BEFORE another past event. Signal words: "by the time," "before," "after...had," "when...already."
Present perfect (has/have + verb)
Use for actions from the past that continue or are relevant to the present. Signal words: "since," "for the past X years," "over the past decade."
Consistency
Stay in the same tense unless the timeline demands a shift. An unexplained switch from past to present mid-passage is always an error.
Sequence rule
"After" and "before" clarify sequence — past perfect is optional with these words. Without sequence words, past perfect is required to show which event came first.

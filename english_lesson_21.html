Concept 21 / 27SEC — Grammar
Subject-Verb
Agreement
The verb must agree with its subject in number — singular subjects take singular verbs, plural subjects take plural verbs. The SAT hides the subject under layers of intervening phrases to make the wrong verb feel right.
~2–3 per moduleTarget: 35 secMethod: cross out everything between subject and verb
S↔V
The rules
Step 1
Core rule

Singular subject → singular verb. Plural subject → plural verb.

"The scientist (singular) studies (singular) coral reefs."
"The scientists (plural) study (plural) coral reefs."

5 tricky subject types

1. Collective nouns (team, committee, board, government, jury, staff) → singular:
"The team has reached a decision." NOT "have."

2. Intervening phrases — cross out everything between subject and verb:
"The quality of the results was surprising." (subject = quality, not results)

3. Neither/nor and either/or — verb agrees with the CLOSER subject:
"Neither the manager nor the employees were informed." (closer = employees, plural)
"Neither the employees nor the manager was informed." (closer = manager, singular)

4. Indefinite pronouns — always singular:
Everyone, everyone, anyone, someone, nobody, each, every, either, neither → singular verb

5. Inverted sentences — verb before subject:
"There were three reasons for the delay." (subject = reasons, plural)

⭐
Strategy
Step 2
1
Find the true subject. Cross out all prepositional phrases, relative clauses, and parenthetical insertions between subject and verb.
2
Identify: singular or plural? Apply the tricky subject rules above if needed.
3
Match the verb. Singular subject → verb ending in -s (he runs, she studies). Plural subject → verb without -s (they run, they study).
⚠️
Traps
Step 3
Trap 1
Agreeing with the nearest noun
"The impact of the new policies were felt immediately." Closest noun = policies (plural). But the subject = impact (singular). Correct: "was felt."
Trap 2
Collective nouns
"The jury have reached a verdict." Jury is collective = singular. Correct: "The jury has reached a verdict."
Trap 3
Indefinite pronouns
"Everyone in both groups were surveyed." Everyone = singular. Correct: "Everyone...was surveyed." The plural "both groups" is just an intervening phrase.
Trap 4
There is / There are
"There was three factors..." The subject follows "there" — "factors" is plural. Correct: "There were three factors." The verb must agree with the real subject, not with "there."

Worked examples
Step 4
Example 1 — Intervening phraseMedium
The effectiveness of the three new conservation measures implemented across the national parks have been questioned by several independent researchers.
  • A. No change
  • B. has been
  • C. were
  • D. are
B. Cross out: "of the three new conservation measures implemented across the national parks" — all intervening. Subject = "The effectiveness" (singular). Singular → "has been." A (have) agrees with "measures" or "parks" — the nearest nouns, not the true subject.
Example 2 — Neither/norHard
Neither the department heads nor the chief executive were available to comment on the allegations at the time of publication.
  • A. No change
  • B. was
  • C. have been
  • D. are
B. Neither/nor rule: agree with the subject CLOSER to the verb. "Chief executive" is singular and is closer to the verb than "department heads." Singular → "was." A (were) would be correct if "chief executive" were plural or if "department heads" were closer to the verb.
Example 3 — Inverted sentenceMedium
Among the findings of the decade-long study was several unexpected correlations between diet and sleep quality.
  • A. No change
  • B. were
  • C. has been
  • D. is
B. Inverted sentence — subject comes AFTER verb. The real subject is "several unexpected correlations" (plural). Plural → "were." A (was) agrees with "study" — the nearest noun before the verb, not the true subject.

Practice — 5 questions
Step 5
Practice 1
The committee of senior advisors have unanimously recommended that the project be delayed until the budget review is complete.
  • A. No change
  • B. has
  • C. had
  • D. were
B. "Committee" = collective noun = singular. Cross out "of senior advisors." Subject = "The committee" (singular) → "has." The word "unanimously" reinforces that the committee acts as one unit.
Practice 2
Each of the twelve scientists who participated in the landmark genome study were recognised at the annual conference.
  • A. No change
  • B. was
  • C. have been
  • D. are
B. "Each" = indefinite pronoun = always singular. Cross out "of the twelve scientists who participated in the landmark genome study." Subject = "Each" (singular) → "was." The plural "twelve scientists" is an intervening phrase, not the subject.
Practice 3
Neither the principal investigator nor the graduate students was aware of the error until the paper had already been submitted for peer review.
  • A. No change
  • B. were
  • C. has been
  • D. is
B. Neither/nor rule: agree with the closer subject. "Graduate students" (plural) is closer to the verb → "were." A (was) would agree with "principal investigator" (singular) — the farther subject.
Practice 4
The data collected from over forty years of atmospheric monitoring suggest that average global temperatures have risen by approximately 1.2°C since pre-industrial levels.
  • A. No change — the sentence is correct
  • B. suggests
  • C. is suggesting
  • D. has suggested
A. "Data" is a plural noun (singular = "datum"). Cross out "collected from over forty years of atmospheric monitoring." Subject = "The data" (plural) → "suggest." B (suggests) treats "data" as singular — a common error. A is correct.
Practice 5 — Hard
Among the qualities that distinguish great leaders from merely competent ones is the ability to inspire others during moments of uncertainty.
  • A. No change — the sentence is correct
  • B. are
  • C. were
  • D. have been
A. Inverted sentence — subject follows the verb. True subject = "the ability to inspire others during moments of uncertainty" (singular — one ability). Singular → "is." B (are) would agree with "qualities" — the nearest noun before the verb, not the true subject that follows.

Summary card
Keep this
Subject-Verb Agreement — Everything you need
Step 1 always
Cross out everything between subject and verb. Find the true subject. Identify singular or plural. Match the verb.
Collective nouns = singular
team, committee, board, government, jury, staff, audience, faculty → singular verb.
Neither/nor · Either/or
Verb agrees with the subject CLOSER to the verb. Closer singular → singular verb. Closer plural → plural verb.
Indefinite pronouns = singular
Everyone, anyone, someone, nobody, each, every, either, neither → always singular verb.
Inverted sentences
Subject follows verb in sentences starting with "There is/are" or prepositional phrases. Find the real subject AFTER the verb.
Data = plural
"Data suggest" not "data suggests." Similarly: criteria (plural of criterion), phenomena (plural of phenomenon).
